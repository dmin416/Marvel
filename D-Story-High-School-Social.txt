How Girls Form Close Bonds at 14 to 18

* A small inner circle: most girls keep two or three close friends inside a wider circle from classes and activities. The inner circle is where trust and secrets live
* Identity: these years are about trying on looks, music and crowds to find out who they are
* Belonging and standing out: girls this age want both at once. A rebellious girl still wants a group, just one that matches her
* Status: who sits where and who is seen with whom matters. Being seen with the wrong person costs something, especially at 14 to 15. By 16 to 17 confident girls care less
* Bonding with girls: closeness grows through talk and confiding
* Bonding with boys: closeness grows through doing things together, such as lab work, a band, a club or a shared problem, along with banter. Most close boy-girl friendships at this age start as working relationships
* Romance: at 14 to 15 girls often look at older boys. Real interest in boys their own age usually grows around 16 to 17, often out of an existing friendship
* Late-1990s channels: landline calls, notes, AOL Instant Messenger, the library, pizza places, the mall and small all-ages shows

What Is Specific to Gwen

* Smart: honors classes put her with the science kids by default. She respects competence and has little patience for people who coast
* Cop's daughter: she is guarded and tests people first. She hates being treated as the captain's daughter or as a prize
* Her father's protectiveness: boys who are pushy or eager to impress lose points fast
* Rebellious but responsible: she is drawn to people who do their own thing without making a show of it
* Easiest ways in: be reliable, do not talk down to her, do not hit on her and let her be good at things

When Gwen Interacts More With Peter and D (Proposed Stages)

1. Freshman fall: thin contact. She is with her middle-school friends and they are strangers who overlap in shared classes. The played details fit this stage: the girl who shared a book and pointed out the library's back tables and the cafeteria-corner study group
2. Lab partners: the most natural first real contact, as in Spider-Man 3 and The Amazing Spider-Man. The teacher assigns pairs, so nobody has to make the first move and no status is at stake
3. Study groups and competitions: library back tables, science fair and academic contests. Sustained time on shared work is how she builds trust with boys
4. Lunch: this comes later. Early on she sits with her own friends. A mixed table usually forms after a class or activity gives them a reason, often sophomore year. A cafeteria table is a public statement, so it follows the friendship rather than starting it
5. Outside school: bands and shows. Possibly Sunday dinners at the Parkers' or F.E.A.S.T. once she is close to the group

Self Defense Club (Idea)

* Open: whether D starts one
* Realism: a student club in 1999 needs a faculty advisor, usually a gym teacher or coach. It also needs enough members to be approved. Late freshman or early sophomore year is realistic
* Peter joins because Flash bullies him. He can join and still stay weak, which matches D-Story-Speed-and-Skill-Over-Time.txt. He might keep showing up out of loyalty and get thrown a lot
* Harry joins because Peter is there and because he trains just enough to impress girls
* Gwen's reasons to join: friends are in it, she gets to throw boys who underestimate her, she can help teach what her father drilled into her and it gives her a way into the group without admitting she wants one
* Gwen's reason not to: she already gets it at home and might roll her eyes at a student club. Middle path: she comes once to prove a point and keeps coming
* Hook: Captain Stacy as a guest speaker. Gwen is mortified and every boy in the room is now terrified of her father
* Lorna could come in through the club too
* Why it matters: the club gives the five of them a regular, legitimate reason to be in one room every week. That lets the lab partner, lunch table and dinner stages happen naturally
