# Marvel Science — Technology, Materials, Electricity and Plant Powers

**Status:** Working worldbuilding document  
**Updated:** 2026-10-31  

## Technology — Iron Spider Arms, Perfect Motors & 6 GW Reactor

**Setting:** Tony Stark–class wearable arc-reactor technology; Iron Spider appendages and Doctor Octopus–style tentacles. **Fictional assumptions:** indestructible arms and direct-drive motors, perfectly conducting wiring, and **6 GW continuous electrical output** from a compact, wearable toroidal fusion reactor.

## Actuators: speed, strength, accuracy

- **Real-world baseline:** brushless permanent-magnet servos; direct drive for low backlash and precise control; low-ratio geared servos for compact torque; high-reduction drives for heavy lifting.
- **Marvel upgrade:** indestructible direct-drive motors and limbs, zero electrical resistance, high-speed encoders, force sensors and distributed predictive controllers. No gearbox is necessary.
- **Precision:** a 20-bit encoder resolves ~0.000343° per count, equivalent to ~6 micrometers at a 1 m radius *ideally*; real tip accuracy still depends on flex, vibrations, calibration and control.
- **Speed:** a 3 m arm moving at 10 rad/s has a tip speed of 30 m/s; at 100 rad/s, **300 m/s**, approaching the speed of sound. Surviving the forces does not remove inertia or air resistance.
- **Strength:** actual torque depends on magnetic field, motor dimensions and current. Six gigawatts alone does **not** guarantee millions of tons of force.

## What 6 GW enables

**6 GW = 6 billion joules per second ≈ 8.05 million mechanical horsepower at 100% efficiency.** It is a power *rate*, not an energy reserve or an automatic torque rating.

The fundamental relationship is **P = torque × angular speed**. If a motor actually delivers 6 GW mechanically, the corresponding operating points are:

| Speed | Torque at 6 GW |
|---|---:|
| 100 RPM | ~573 million N·m |
| 1,000 RPM | ~57.3 million N·m |
| 10,000 RPM | ~5.73 million N·m |

These are **mathematical power/torque combinations, not guaranteed motor capabilities**. Multiple arms share the same power budget. A stopped motor can provide holding torque with zero mechanical output power.

## Limits even with indestructible arms and motors

1. **Electromagnetics:** perfectly conducting windings do not eliminate magnetic saturation, field-strength limits, winding inductance, back-EMF or control-voltage requirements. Truly extreme torque needs Marvel-level magnetic materials.
2. **Inertia:** arm mass still requires force to accelerate and stop; momentum and recoil still exist. At 300 m/s, a 100 kg point mass moving in a 3 m-radius circle requires roughly **3 million newtons** of centripetal force (~3,060 g).
3. **Wearer:** an indestructible tentacle can still injure its wearer through sudden loads. Protective mounting, body support or fictional inertial mitigation is needed.
4. **Environment:** anchors, buildings and whatever the arms grip can still fail, even when the arms cannot.
5. **Heat and control:** losses in reactor equipment, magnetic systems, air and surroundings remain; a 1 ms delay at 100 m/s means **10 cm** of tip travel.

## Plasma and energy inertia

- **Plasma has ordinary mass and inertia** because it consists of particles. Its circulating motion can carry angular momentum, but magnetically confined plasma is **not a rigid flywheel**.
- **Example:** 1 gram of plasma circulating at 1,000 km/s around a 15 cm radius has **500 MJ** of kinetic energy and **150 kg·m²/s** of angular momentum under a simple ring model.
- **Stored energy also contributes inertia:** via **m = E/c²**, 6 GJ of *stored* energy has a mass equivalent of ~**66.8 micrograms**. Generating 6 GW does not itself continuously add that mass; only retained energy contributes to the reactor's stored-energy mass.
- Plasma mass can be tiny compared with containment coils, structural equipment and shielding; its own inertia is generally **not** the chief backpack design obstacle.

## Wearable toroidal fusion reactor

**Marvel premise:** Tony Stark wears a compact arc reactor; the Iron Spider suit can use a related or separate wearable power system. Our version is a **backpack- or chest-mounted toroidal fusion system** delivering 6 GW continuously.

| Element | Marvel-world solution |
|---|---|
| Plasma containment | Celestial-derived superconducting nerve material powering extreme magnetic fields |
| Reactor shell, arms and joints | Indestructible Celestial-derived structural materials |
| Electrical delivery | Perfect-conductor power distribution |
| Electricity generation | Fictional highly efficient direct fusion-energy conversion |
| Wearer protection | Advanced radiation shielding, thermal management, load isolation |

**Real-physics caveat:** no demonstrated fusion reactor can currently deliver 6 GW from a backpack. Miniaturized confinement, stability, radiation shielding, power conversion and waste-heat rejection remain major unsolved barriers. Even **0.1% losses at 6 GW = 6 MW of heat**. “Aneutronic” reactions might reduce neutron problems but are harder to achieve and do not eliminate all radiation or heat.

## Bottom line

**In Marvel continuity:** A Stark-style wearable 6 GW toroidal reactor driving indestructible, perfectly conducting direct-drive Iron Spider or Doc Ock arms is a coherent *fictional technology*. The plasma's inertia is comparatively minor. The decisive enhancements are **ultra-strong electromagnetic fields, reliable energy conversion, heat/radiation protection, precise control, and shielding the wearer from reaction forces**. Immense power does not mean infinite torque, speed or precision.

# Marvel Continuity — Celestial Materials and Powers

**Status:** Working worldbuilding document  
**Updated:** 2026-10-31  
**Continuity:** Custom Marvel setting. The biological origins below are proposed setting lore, not established Marvel canon.

## Core premise

The death of a Celestial scatters extraordinary matter and energy across the universe. Different civilizations find the remains, mine them, refine them, and assign different names to the resulting materials. The properties of those materials reflect the biological function of the tissue while the Celestial lived.

The scale of the fight and mode of death determine how much of the body remains intact, how far its components spread, and what happens to its energy and consciousness.

**Design note:** Several material-to-organ assignments are provisional. Preserve the distinction between naturally occurring Celestial tissue and a material that must be produced through metallurgy or another refinement process.

## Superconducting nerves — established decision

- Celestial nerves are superconductors.
- Their normal function is to carry vast electrical currents and nerve signals with essentially no resistive loss under operating conditions.
- Preserved nerve trunks form potentially immense superconducting cables; severed pieces yield shorter filaments; shattered nerve tissue yields microscopic inclusions.
- Intact neural networks may contain traces of recorded signals or activity, but superconductivity alone does not imply telepathy, sentience, or a surviving soul.
- The resulting Marvel-setting material is not yet named.

## Death mechanics

| Manner of death | Result for the body | Possible result for consciousness / energy |
|---|---|---|
| Decapitation | Relatively intact head, perhaps a viable location or mining site | Separated, dormant, trapped, dispersed, or extinguished; case-dependent |
| Dismemberment in battle | Large organs and limbs scattered through space | Energy or traces of thought may remain localized in fragments |
| Catastrophic destruction | Material distributed across many worlds | Enormous release of cosmic energy |
| Gradual death | Slow decay and long-term mineral deposition | Gradual dissipation or persistence |
| Petrification | Vast stable mineralized remains | May be dormant or trapped |
| Mystical or soul-targeting death | Physical remains may persist | Soul may be imprisoned, consumed, destroyed, or otherwise transformed |

Three separate questions apply to any corpse: (1) Does its physical tissue remain? (2) Does the tissue retain extraordinary energy and properties? (3) Does the Celestial’s consciousness or soul survive? The answers can differ.

## Knowhere

In our continuity, the Celestial One Above All was decapitated and its severed head became Knowhere. The head could retain mineable bone, nerve, enamel, and other tissue without necessarily retaining an active mind. This identification is a custom-continuity choice, not a confirmed MCU identification of Knowhere’s Celestial. Do not confuse this Celestial with Marvel’s supreme entity of a similar name.

## Distribution

- Precise deaths may leave concentrated, mineable bodies.
- Violent cosmic battles can scatter fragments between star systems.
- Catastrophic deaths can seed many worlds with trace deposits.
- Multiple Celestials can produce comparable materials if their basic anatomy is shared.
- Individual Celestials may still possess specialized organs and unique products.

# Electricity, Plasma and Lightning — Detailed Reference


## 1. Plasma Arcs

**Plasma basics.** Plasma is gas with electrons stripped off its atoms, so it conducts electricity and reacts to magnetic fields. It glows because electrons recombine with ions and excited atoms release energy as light. Nitrogen and oxygen give arcs in air their blue-violet-white color.

**How air breaks down.** Air insulates until the electric field reaches about 3 million volts per meter (30 kV per cm) at sea level. Then:
- A stray free electron (knocked loose by cosmic rays or natural radioactivity) speeds up in the field.
- It hits a molecule hard enough to free a second electron. Two become four, four become eight. This chain reaction is called the Townsend avalanche.
- The avalanches merge into glowing filaments called streamers.
- Once a streamer crosses the gap, current surges through and heats the path into a conducting channel.

**Paschen's law.** The voltage needed to break down a gas depends on its pressure multiplied by the gap distance. Thinner air sparks more easily, but only down to a point. In near-vacuum there are too few molecules for an avalanche to build. Air has a floor of about 330 volts. Below that, no gap of any size will spark.

**Glow vs. arc.** A glow discharge (like a neon sign) runs at low current and stays cool. An arc runs at high current. The negative electrode (the cathode) gets hot enough to boil electrons out of the metal, a process called thermionic emission. The column of the arc reaches roughly 5,000 to 30,000 °C. The voltage across an arc drops as its current rises. That "negative resistance" makes an arc run away unless something limits the current. This is why arc lamps need ballasts and welders use constant-current power supplies.

**Pinch effect.** Current flowing through an arc creates a magnetic field that wraps around it and squeezes it inward. A large enough current can crush metal tubes. Sandia's Z machine uses this pinch to produce X-rays and fusion conditions.

**Arc flash.** Industrial arc faults reach about 35,000 °F (19,400 °C) at the terminals. Copper boils into vapor and expands about 67,000 times in volume. That launches a pressure wave, a spray of molten metal, blinding UV light and a bang loud enough to rupture eardrums. It is the closest real-world match to a superhero's plasma blast.

**Arc tools:**
- Arc welding
- Plasma cutters: a narrowed arc inside a gas jet, hotter than 20,000 °C, that slices steel
- Electric arc furnaces that melt scrap steel
- Carbon arc searchlights
- Jacob's ladders

## 2. Lightning

**Charging the cloud.** Updrafts inside a cumulonimbus storm cloud carry water up into the zone between about -10 and -20 °C. There, supercooled droplets, ice crystals and soft hail pellets (graupel) mix and collide. The collisions swap charge. Light ice crystals turn positive and ride the updraft to the top while the heavier graupel turns negative and stays in the middle. The result is a positive top, a main negative layer in the middle and a small positive pocket near the base.

**The initiation puzzle.** Fields measured inside storms top out around 100,000 to 400,000 V/m. That is far short of the 3 million needed to break down air. Two explanations carry the most weight. Ice particles concentrate the field at their tips. Cosmic rays also seed very fast "runaway" electrons that can avalanche at much lower fields.

**Stepped leader.** A faint negative channel creeps toward the ground in jumps about 50 m long. It pauses for tens of microseconds between steps and branches as it goes. Its average speed is about 200 km/s.

**Connection.** As the leader nears the ground, the field around trees, towers, rooftops and people spikes until they launch upward streamers. When one of those streamers meets the leader, usually tens of meters up, the circuit closes. Tall pointed objects launch streamers first, which is why they get hit.

**Return stroke.** This is the flash you actually see: a wave of current racing up the channel at a third to half the speed of light.
- Peak current: about 30,000 A typically. Positive strikes can top 200,000 A.
- Voltage between cloud and ground: 100 million to 1 billion volts
- Channel temperature: about 30,000 K (50,000 °F), five times hotter than the Sun's surface
- Width of the hot core: a few centimeters
- Energy: about 1 billion joules per flash

**Multiple strokes.** After the first stroke, "dart leaders" race back down the still-hot channel and trigger new return strokes. A flash averages 3 to 4 strokes over 0.2 to 0.5 seconds, which is why lightning flickers. Some flashes keep carrying current (hundreds of amps for a tenth of a second or longer). Those flashes start most lightning fires.

**Types.**
- **Intracloud:** flashes within a cloud, about three quarters of all lightning
- **Negative cloud-to-ground:** most ground strikes
- **Positive cloud-to-ground:** under 10% of ground strikes. They launch from the flat anvil top of the storm, with bigger currents that last longer. They are the source of "bolts from the blue" that land 10+ miles from the storm.
- **Upward lightning:** launched from skyscrapers and towers
- **Ball lightning:** glowing spheres that drift for seconds. A 2012 recording of its light spectrum in China showed silicon, iron and calcium. That supports the theory that it is vaporized soil burning in the air.
- **Above the storm:**
  - Red sprites, 50 to 90 km up
  - Elves: expanding rings of glow hundreds of km wide at the edge of the ionosphere
  - Blue jets and gigantic jets that reach the ionosphere
- **Other:** volcanic lightning from colliding ash, and thundersnow in winter storms

**Exotic output.** Thunderstorms fire bursts of gamma rays. In 2011 NASA's Fermi telescope detected beams of antimatter (positrons) coming from storms. Lightning also:
- turns nitrogen in the air into natural fertilizer
- makes ozone
- fuses sand into glass tubes called fulgurites
- leaves fern-shaped marks called Lichtenberg figures on the skin of people it strikes

**Global scale.** Earth gets about 44 flashes per second, around 1.4 billion a year. Together, the world's storms keep the ionosphere charged about 250,000 volts above the ground (the "global electric circuit"). That gives calm air a standing field of about 100 V/m.

**How strikes hurt people.**
- **Ground current** causes about half of lightning casualties. Current spreads outward from the strike point, so the voltage difference between a person's spread feet drives current up one leg and down the other. Whole herds of cattle die this way because their four legs stand far apart.
- **Other causes:** side flashes from struck objects, touching something that gets struck, upward streamers and direct strikes.
- **Survival:** about 90% of victims survive. Cardiac arrest and nerve damage are what kill.

**Harnessing it.** A billion joules is only about 280 kWh, roughly nine days of power for an average US home. It also arrives in microseconds at random spots. Most of it turns into heat, light and sound in the air before reaching the ground. Capturing it would need storage that could absorb gigawatts instantly.

Researchers trigger lightning on purpose by launching small rockets trailing grounded wire into charged clouds (at Camp Blanding, Florida). In experiments published in 2023, a team on Switzerland's Säntis mountain fired a high-power laser into storms. The laser created an ionized path, and lightning followed it for about 50 m.

## 3. Thunder and Concussive Force

**The boom.** The return stroke dumps its energy into the channel within microseconds, faster than the air can move out of the way. Pressure inside the channel spikes to 10 atmospheres or more and the channel explodes outward faster than sound. Thunder starts life as a shock wave.

**Blast zone.** Within roughly the first 10 m, the shock wave hits like an explosion. Ruptured eardrums are among the most common lightning injuries. People near a strike get knocked flat, partly by the blast and partly by violent full-body muscle contractions from the current. Beyond about 10 m, the shock fades into ordinary sound.

**The concussion principle.** Concussive force depends on how fast and how tightly energy is released, not just on how much energy there is. A billion joules released over a minute would just warm the air. Released in microseconds along a pencil-thin line, it becomes an explosion. Lab "exploding wire" experiments show the same thing. A capacitor dumps its charge into a wire, and the wire vaporizes into plasma that bursts outward with a crack.

**The rumble.**
- A lightning channel is several kilometers long and full of kinks. Each segment sits at a different distance from you, so the sound arrives drawn out over time.
- A segment that runs broadside to you delivers all its sound at once as a sharp clap.
- Air absorbs high frequencies over distance, so close strikes crack while distant ones rumble.
- Echoes off hills and buildings stretch the sound further.
- Clouds also give off low infrasound, below human hearing, as their charge drains away.

**Distance and loudness.** Sound travels about 343 m/s: 3 seconds per kilometer, or 5 seconds per mile. Close strikes are louder than 120 dB. Thunder rarely carries past 10 to 15 miles because air gets colder with height, which bends sound upward over listeners' heads. "Heat lightning" is just ordinary lightning too far away to hear.

## 4. Max Dillon (Electro)

**Versions.**
- **Comics:** debuted in *Amazing Spider-Man* #9 (1964). Max is a power lineman struck by lightning while working on a line, which turns his body into a living capacitor.
- **The Amazing Spider-Man 2 (2014):** Jamie Foxx plays an Oscorp electrical engineer. He gets shocked while fixing a live cable and falls into a tank of genetically engineered electric eels. He becomes a being of electricity who can dissolve into energy, travel through the power grid and drain Times Square. Spider-Man beats him by feeding him more grid power than he can hold until he overloads.
- **Spider-Man: No Way Home (2021):** the same Max, pulled across the multiverse, powers up on a Stark arc reactor before being cured.

**The eel origin is real biology.** Electric eels stack about 6,000 modified muscle cells (electrocytes) in a row, like batteries. Each cell adds roughly 0.15 V. The species *Electrophorus voltai* reaches 860 V, the highest of any animal. Eels also leap out of the water and press against threats to push more current into them.

**The fuel problem.** One lightning-scale blast (1 billion joules) equals about 239,000 food Calories, around 120 days of eating. A purely biological Electro would starve after a few big shots. This is why every version feeds on the grid, batteries or reactors. He is a conduit and a storage tank, not a power plant.

**Not cooking himself.** Electrical injury comes from current flowing through tissue.
- About 10 to 20 mA of household current (60 Hz) locks the muscles so a victim can't let go.
- About 100 mA across the chest can throw the heart into fibrillation, a chaotic quivering instead of a beat.
- High voltage punches through skin within milliseconds, and then the body's resistance collapses.

To survive his own power, Electro needs one of three things:
- a superconducting body that doesn't heat up
- current flowing along his outside like a Faraday shell
- a body made of plasma

**Living plasma.** The 2014 version partly becomes plasma. Real plasma turns back into ordinary gas within microseconds to milliseconds unless energy keeps flowing in. It also expands and cools unless magnetic fields hold it in place. A plasma body would need its own magnetic bottle, like a fusion reactor's, plus a nonstop power supply. That fits the movie's hunger for the grid.

**Riding the wires.** Electrons in household wiring creep along at a fraction of a millimeter per second. The energy moves through the electromagnetic field around the wire at a large fraction of light speed. An Electro traveling through the grid would be a self-sustaining pattern in that field, not a stream of electrons.

**Aiming.** A raw electrical discharge follows whatever path breaks down most easily. That is why sparks branch wildly and jump to the nearest metal. Aiming one requires ionizing a path first: with UV light, a laser channel like the Säntis experiment, or a conductive stream like water or metal dust. Electro's straight bolts imply he ionizes the air ahead of each strike.

## 5. Doctor Doom, Fantastic Four (2005)

**The film version.** Julian McMahon's Victor Von Doom pays for the space station mission and gets caught in the cosmic storm. The comics' Doom relies on armor and sorcery. The film's Doom gains electrical powers instead: drawing current from power sources, firing bolts from his hands and arcing current through anyone he grabs. The storm also slowly turns his body into organic metal.

**Why metal skin fits.** A conductive metal shell acts as a Faraday cage: current flows along the outside instead of through what's inside. Cars protect passengers from lightning the same way (the metal body does it, not the rubber tires). Doom's metal skin works as a built-in lightning shield. It also gives him a low-resistance path for pouring huge currents into whatever he touches.

**The defeat.** The team beats him with thermal shock. Johnny goes supernova to superheat him, then they drench him with water from a fire hydrant. The sudden cooling freezes him into a metal statue. Real metalworking supports the idea. Plunging red-hot steel into water ("quenching") traps carbon in a hard, brittle crystal structure called martensite. Quenching thick metal too hard can crack it outright.

**Comics contrast.** The classic comics Doom has no electric power of his own. His armor shocks anyone who touches it, fires energy blasts and projects force fields. The 2005 film moved those armor features into his body.

## 6. Storm

**Her advantage.** Ororo Munroe debuted in *Giant-Size X-Men* #1 (1975). She doesn't generate power from her own body. She commands the atmosphere, which already holds staggering amounts of energy. According to NOAA, a mature hurricane releases about 6×10¹⁴ watts of heat as its water vapor condenses. That is more than 100 times humanity's total electricity output. Storm steers an existing power source instead of having to fuel one.

**Building lightning the natural way.**
1. Push warm, moist air upward with wind and low pressure.
2. As the moisture condenses, it releases heat that strengthens the updraft.
3. Lift moisture into the -10 to -20 °C zone so graupel and ice collide and separate charge.
4. Concentrate the field over the target.

Nature needs 20 minutes or more to build a thunderstorm. Storm does it in seconds, which implies she moves charged particles directly, the same way she moves air.

**Feats and the physics behind them.**
- **Clear-sky lightning:** separating charge directly, with no cloud needed
- **Precision strikes:** steering the stepped leader or ionizing a channel ahead of time
- **Indoor lightning:** separating charge inside the air of a single room
- **Flight:** riding wind currents she creates
- **Wet-target combos:** rain or fog first, so current spreads across skin and surfaces
- **Crowd control:** striking wet ground near a group so ground current drops everyone at once
- **Combined weather:** hail, freezing temperatures, pressure drops and tornado-like vortices alongside lightning

## 7. Every Way to Use Electricity

**Offense**
- **Lightning bolt:** heat, current and shock wave in one hit
- **Contact shock:** locks muscles so the target can't let go
- **Nerve override:** pulsed current hijacks the nerves that move muscles, which is how stun guns drop people
- **Heart stop:** current across the chest triggers fibrillation
- **Thunderclap:** dump energy into a small pocket of air within microseconds for a pure concussive blast
- **Flash blind:** arc light causes flash blindness and "welder's eye," a UV burn on the cornea
- **Exploding metal:** overload a fence, chain or rebar until it vaporizes into a burst of plasma
- **Plasma blade:** a narrowed arc plus airflow cuts steel, like a plasma cutter
- **Induction heating:** rapidly alternating magnetic fields make metal heat itself (how induction stoves work), turning guns, armor or cars red-hot from across a room
- **Railgun/coilgun:** magnetic force launches coins, bolts or rebar at kilometers per second
- **Electromagnetism:** lift cars, rip away weapons, pin armored enemies
- **EMP:** sudden changes in the field induce power surges that fry electronics
- **Sonic attack:** modulate the arc to make sound (singing Tesla coils and plasma speakers do this)
- **Radiation:** avalanches of very fast electrons give off X-rays and gamma rays, the same way storms do

**Defense**
- **Faraday skin:** route current around the body instead of through it
- **Eddy-current braking:** strong magnetic fields drag on moving metal (why a magnet falls slowly through a copper pipe), slowing metal projectiles
- **Magnetic deflection:** push steel projectiles off course or bend beams of charged particles
- **Plasma wall:** a sheet of hot plasma burns away incoming debris. Real "plasma windows" use arcs to hold a vacuum apart from open air.
- **Electrostatic screen:** charge smoke, dust or gas particles and pull them out of the air, like smokestack filters do
- **Grounding:** redirect incoming electric attacks into the earth

**Movement**
- **Power line travel:** move as a pattern in the grid's field
- **Magnetic levitation:** induce currents in metal surfaces below and push off them (Lenz's law, the same principle some maglev trains use)
- **Ion wind:** high voltage ionizes air and pushes it backward silently. In 2018 MIT flew a 5 m plane on ion thrust with no moving parts.
- **Plasma thrust:** Hall-effect and ion thrusters already push spacecraft today
- **Electroadhesion:** electric charge sticks to walls, which real wall-climbing robots use

**Senses**
- **Electroreception:** sharks detect fields as weak as a few billionths of a volt per centimeter. That is enough to sense heartbeats, hidden people and buried wires.
- **Electrolocation:** weakly electric fish broadcast a field and read its distortions to "see" in murky water. The same trick would map a dark room.
- **Radio and radar:** transmit and receive, listen to broadcasts, jam communications, echolocate with radio waves
- **Device reading:** electronics leak electromagnetic signals that reveal what they are doing
- **Storm sense:** feel the electric field building before a strike

**Tech control**
- **Power:** charge phones, jump-start cars, light a city block
- **Drain:** kill batteries, black out a grid
- **Overload:** spike the voltage to blow breakers, transformers and circuits
- **Wireless power:** resonant induction (the Tesla coil principle) powers devices across a room
- **Signal injection:** data is just patterns of voltage, so controlling the voltage means controlling the device
- **Degaussing:** wipe magnetic storage, and magnetize or demagnetize metal

**Medicine and the body**
- **Defibrillation:** a controlled 120 to 200 J shock resets a fibrillating heart
- **Pacing:** small rhythmic pulses keep a heart beating
- **Cauterizing:** electrosurgery uses high-frequency current to cut tissue and seal bleeding vessels
- **Pain blocking:** TENS units scramble pain signals
- **Puppeteering:** electrical stimulation makes paralyzed muscles move. Scaled up, it could move someone else's limbs.
- **Brain stimulation:** transcranial magnetic stimulation activates specific brain regions through the skull
- **Bone healing:** electrical stimulation speeds up fracture repair
- **Sterilizing:** UV light and ozone from arcs kill microbes

**Matter and industry**
- **Weld, cut, melt:** arc welding, plasma cutting, arc furnaces
- **Electrolysis:**
  - split water into breathable oxygen (plus hydrogen)
  - coat objects in metal (electroplating)
  - refine aluminum (nearly all new aluminum is smelted with electricity)
- **Fertilizer from air:** the Birkeland-Eyde process of the early 1900s used giant arcs to turn nitrogen in the air into fertilizer
- **Glassmaking:** fuse sand into fulgurites
- **Light and heat:** arc lamps, glowing gas, and electrical resistance heating to boil water or start fires
- **Origin of life:** the 1952 Miller-Urey experiment ran sparks through a mix of gases like Earth's early atmosphere and produced amino acids

**Exotic**
- **Pinch fusion:** squeeze plasma with its own magnetic field until it reaches fusion conditions, as Sandia's Z machine does
- **Particle beams:** accelerate electrons into a tight beam
- **Antimatter:** avalanches of very fast electrons create positrons, as storms do
- **Plasma constructs:** ball-lightning-style orbs held together by magnetic fields
- **Planet-scale tapping:** draw on the 250,000-volt global circuit between the ground and the ionosphere
- **Ionosphere heating:** pumping radio energy into the upper atmosphere, as research arrays like HAARP do, disrupts long-range radio

**Countering electric powers**
- **Grounding:** rods and chains give current a path around its targets
- **Faraday cages:** metal mesh enclosures keep current on the outside
- **Overload:** the 2014 film's solution, more power than the body can hold
- **Thermal shock:** the 2005 film's solution, superheat then quench
- **Arc-killing gas:** high-voltage circuit breakers are filled with sulfur hexafluoride (SF6) because it captures free electrons and snuffs out arcs almost instantly
- **Vacuum:** vacuum circuit breakers put out arcs quickly by removing the gas that feeds them
- **Magnetic blowout:** magnets stretch arcs until they break, a technique used in DC breakers
- **Pure water and oil:** distilled water and transformer oil insulate. Dirty water conducts.
- **Distance:** a long spark in air still needs roughly 500,000 volts for every meter it crosses, so distance gets expensive for an electric attacker

## NovaCorp applied science priorities — selective future program

**Aim:** Turn breakthrough science into widespread, rigorously tested improvements in living conditions. Prioritize results over collecting patents. Shadow can identify promising paths, but human teams, reproducibility, engineering, clinical evidence and public institutions still matter.

**Materials and industry:** Higher-efficiency power electronics, better batteries and recycling, corrosion-resistant and repairable materials, safe filtration membranes, precision manufacturing, and lower-impact industrial chemistry. **Research-to-use sequence:** Choose high-impact problems → establish prior work and valid rights → test a reproducible prototype → independent safety review → permits/clinical trials where required → scalable manufacturing → affordable distribution and real-world outcome monitoring.
