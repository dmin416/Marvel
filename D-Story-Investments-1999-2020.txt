D Story: Investments 1999 to 2020

Note: This is my own version of the story and its characters. Marvel and other works are inspirations only. References to canon, Raimi or other sources describe the inspiration and are not fixed facts of this story unless a file marks them as approved.

Status: planning reference. This is real-world market history for D's foreknowledge. The only story canon is D-Story-So-Far.txt.

Accuracy note: the figures below are sourced and each one says where it came from. Published rankings use different start and end dates and most exclude dividends, so numbers for the same company vary a lot between sources. Where a figure is my own arithmetic the method is shown so it can be rechecked.

Monster Beverage, Calculated to End of 2020

Method, using year-end as-traded prices from 1stock1 and the split list from the same page:

* Year-end 1999 price: $4.3125, as traded
* Splits between 2000 and the end of 2020: 2-for-1 in 2005, 4-for-1 in 2006, 2-for-1 in 2012 and 3-for-1 in 2016. That multiplies one share into 48
* Year-end 2020 price: $92.48
* One share at $4.3125 becomes 48 shares worth $4,439, which is about 1,030 times the money, or roughly a 103,000% gain
* Cross-check: the same method at the October 2019 price gives about 63,000%, which matches the anchor list's 62,444% closely. That is why the method is trusted here
* Monster pays no dividend, so price return is close to total return
* A later 2023 article from the Motley Fool says 116,700% since the start of 2000, which fits the same curve extended by three more years

Amazon, the Surprise

* Year-end 1999 price: $456.75, split-adjusted for its 1999 splits (1stock1)
* Year-end 2020 price: $3,256.93. Amazon had no splits between 2000 and 2020; the 20-for-1 came in 2022
* That is about 7.1 times the money, or roughly a 613% gain over twenty-one years
* Amazon is the single most important lesson in this file. Buying the most famous internet company at the end of 1999 returned about 7x over two decades, while a canned drinks company returned about 1,030x
* The reason is the entry price. End of 1999 was near Amazon's bubble peak. It then fell about 80% in 2000 and about 30% in 2001 before recovering

The Long-Run Ordering

Source: totalrealreturns.com, $10,000 invested on January 22, 1999, dividends reinvested, measured to October 2026. Useful for relative ranking only, since the end date is past the story window.

* Nvidia: about $56.6 million
* Monster Beverage: about $18.4 million
* Apple: about $12.0 million
* Amazon: about $814,000

Nvidia went public on January 22, 1999, so it is buyable from the story's first months. Most of its rise came after 2016 and especially after 2022, which is outside the 1999-to-2020 window.

The Crash Is the Real Knowledge

* The Nasdaq peaked at 5,048.62 on March 10, 2000 and bottomed at 1,114.11 on October 9, 2002, a fall of about 78%
* Cisco did not close above its March 2000 high until about December 2025, roughly 25 years later. Its market value is still well below the bubble peak because of share buybacks
* Intel took roughly a quarter century to clear its 2000 peak
* Microsoft was still down about 44% from its peak in 2020 and was the only one of the five biggest dot-com names to beat the S&P 500 over the following two decades
* So the valuable foreknowledge in September 1999 is not a list of companies. It is knowing to sell in early 2000 and buy again in October 2002 and March 2009

Other Markets

* Gold ran from a few hundred dollars an ounce around 1999 to far higher by its 2011 peak
* Oil peaked in 2008 and crashed, then crashed again in 2020
* U.S. housing peaked around 2006 and collapsed. Shorting mortgage securities in 2007 was the trade of the decade and is not available to a retail investor
* Bitcoin exists from 2009 and is a different kind of answer entirely

The Real Constraint for the Story

D's problem in September 1999 is not knowing what to buy:

* He is fourteen. A minor could not open a brokerage account alone in 1999. He needs a custodial account with an adult, which means telling an adult something
* He has no money. He lives in a children's home, so the first stake has to come from somewhere. That is a story problem worth playing rather than skipping
* Trading in 1999 means a phone call to a broker or a dial-up account, a commission on every trade and no instant information
* Explosive returns take decades. Monster's run is a twenty-year hold, not a quick score
* Sudden unexplained wealth draws attention from the home, the school, the IRS and anyone watching
* Picking only winners for twenty years looks like insider trading to anyone who checks

Story Uses

* A small, patient, boring portfolio explains the wealth D and Harry have by 2012 without anyone being able to prove anything
* Selling in early 2000 and buying back in late 2002 is a cleaner demonstration of foreknowledge than any single pick and the one a reader will recognize
* Monster Beverage is the perfect joke pick. A fortune made on canned energy drinks while everyone else chased the internet
* The 2008 crisis is where D can look like a genius and a villain at once, since he profits while the city suffers
* The money funds the courier company, the labs and Oscorp's turn toward medicine (see D-Story-World-Economy.txt)
