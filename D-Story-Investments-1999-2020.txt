D Story: Investments 1999 to 2020

Note: This is my own version of the story and its characters. Marvel and other works are inspirations only. References to canon, Raimi or other sources describe the inspiration and are not fixed facts of this story unless a file marks them as approved.

Status: planning reference. Part one is real-world market history for D's foreknowledge. Part two is where this world's markets stop matching reality. The only story canon is D-Story-So-Far.txt.

Accuracy note: the figures below are sourced and each one says where it came from. Published rankings use different start and end dates and most exclude dividends, so numbers for the same company vary a lot between sources. Where a figure is my own arithmetic the method is shown so it can be rechecked.

How Much of Reality Applies

* For ordinary people and ordinary companies this world is exactly the real 1999 (see Technology Level in D-Story-World-Economy.txt). Dial-up internet, Pentium III computers, no smartphones, oil and gas energy, the real dot-com boom
* The advances in this world sit locked inside Oscorp, Stark, Pym, S.H.I.E.L.D., Wakanda and Von Doom Industries. None of them share or publish. So nothing leaks into the wider economy and the public market behaves like the real one
* That means real market history is usable, with real companies, real prices and real crashes, from 1999 up to spring 2018
* After the Snap in spring 2018 nothing real applies. Half of all life vanishing ends the usefulness of any real figure
* Oscorp, Stark Industries, Hammer Industries, Von Doom Industries and Roxxon are this world's own public companies. Their prices are a story matter and not a real one

Part One: Real Market History, 1999 to 2018

Everything in this part is real and usable up to spring 2018. Figures with later end dates are marked.

1. Monster Beverage: 62,444%
2. Netflix: 23,071% (IPO 2002, so measured from the IPO)
3. Equinix: 12,050%
4. Tractor Supply Company: 10,171%
5. Intuitive Surgical: 9,155%
6. Ansys: 7,856%
7. Apple: 7,416%
8. IDEXX Laboratories: 6,822%
9. Mastercard: 6,279% (IPO 2006, so measured from the IPO)
10. Ross Stores: 6,003%

Notes on this list:

* It is the closest published dataset to the 1999-to-2020 window with a fixed start and end date. Its October 2019 end date falls after this world's Snap, so the last eighteen months of it do not apply here
* The article states the same number as both a percentage and a dollar value for $100, which is internally inconsistent by a factor of about 100. Treat the ranking order as solid and the exact percentages as approximate
* Netflix and Mastercard are measured from their IPOs, so a 1999 investor could not have bought them at all
* Equinix, Ansys and IDEXX Laboratories are the three names almost nobody would guess

Monster Beverage, Calculated to End of 2020

Method, using year-end as-traded prices from 1stock1 and the split list from the same page:

* Year-end 1999 price: $4.3125, as traded
* Splits between 2000 and the end of 2020: 2-for-1 in 2005, 4-for-1 in 2006, 2-for-1 in 2012 and 3-for-1 in 2016. That multiplies one share into 48
* Year-end 2020 price: $92.48
* One share at $4.3125 becomes 48 shares worth $4,439, which is about 1,030 times the money, or roughly a 103,000% gain
* Cross-check: the same method at the October 2019 price gives about 63,000%, which matches the anchor list's 62,444% closely. That is why the method is trusted here
* Monster pays no dividend, so price return is close to total return
* A later 2023 article from the Motley Fool says 116,700% since the start of 2000, which fits the same curve extended by three more years

Amazon, the Surprise

* Year-end 1999 price: $456.75, split-adjusted for its 1999 splits (1stock1)
* Year-end 2020 price: $3,256.93. Amazon had no splits between 2000 and 2020; the 20-for-1 came in 2022
* That is about 7.1 times the money, or roughly a 613% gain over twenty-one years
* Amazon is the single most important lesson in this file. Buying the most famous internet company at the end of 1999 returned about 7x over two decades, while a canned drinks company returned about 1,030x
* The reason is the entry price. End of 1999 was near Amazon's bubble peak. It then fell about 80% in 2000 and about 30% in 2001 before recovering

The Long-Run Ordering

Source: totalrealreturns.com, $10,000 invested on January 22, 1999, dividends reinvested, measured to October 2026. Useful for relative ranking only, since the end date is past the story window.

* Nvidia: about $56.6 million
* Monster Beverage: about $18.4 million
* Apple: about $12.0 million
* Amazon: about $814,000

Nvidia went public on January 22, 1999, so it is buyable from the story's first months. Most of its rise came after 2016 and especially after 2022, which is outside the 1999-to-2020 window.

The Crash Is the Real Knowledge

* The Nasdaq peaked at 5,048.62 on March 10, 2000 and bottomed at 1,114.11 on October 9, 2002, a fall of about 78%
* Cisco did not close above its March 2000 high until about December 2025, roughly 25 years later. Its market value is still well below the bubble peak because of share buybacks
* Intel took roughly a quarter century to clear its 2000 peak
* Microsoft was still down about 44% from its peak in 2020 and was the only one of the five biggest dot-com names to beat the S&P 500 over the following two decades
* So the valuable foreknowledge in September 1999 is not a list of companies. It is knowing to sell in early 2000 and buy again in October 2002 and March 2009

* U.S. housing peaked around 2006 and collapsed. Shorting mortgage securities in 2007 was the trade of the decade and is not available to a retail investor
* Bitcoin exists from 2009 and is a different kind of answer entirely

Part Two: Where This World Stops Matching Reality

This World's Own Stocks

* Oscorp: a New York chemicals company moving into military biotechnology, with an Army contract in 1999 (Played). Its price should track the contracts, Norman's madness, the board's deaths, the takeover and then the turn toward medicine. The 2008 scandal is a crash. Harry's rebuild and the medicine pivot is a long climb
* Stark Industries: the largest U.S. weapons maker. In spring 2008 Tony is captured and then shuts down weapons manufacturing, which crashes the stock. The arc reactor and clean energy rebuild it. By 2012 the company is in a different business entirely
* Hammer Industries: a second-tier contractor. It rises on the contracts Stark abandons in 2008 and collapses after the Stark Expo drone disaster in spring 2010 and Justin Hammer's arrest
* Von Doom Industries: aerospace, buying American suppliers in 1999 (Played) and planning a private orbital platform. The failed space station mission and the cosmic storm wreck it. Victor's board then pushes him out
* Roxxon: oil and gas, which tracks real oil prices
* Pym Technologies: Hank's own company. Darren Cross and Hope van Dyne push him out. Cross then tries to sell the Yellowjacket suit in 2015
* None of these have real tickers. Their prices are whatever the story needs

The Event Shocks This World Adds

* Spring 2008, Tony's capture and Stark leaving weapons: Stark crashes, Hammer rises and the whole defense sector reshuffles
* Spring 2010, the Stark Expo drone attack and the Harlem fight: Hammer collapses. Ross brought the Army into a New York neighborhood, which no amount of silence could hide
* May 2012, the Battle of New York: enormous damage to Midtown East. Insurers take huge losses. Construction, demolition, hauling and salvage boom. Property near the damage sells cheap and then recovers. D's company wins the Chitauri salvage contract and D and Harry take most of what would have been Fisk's monopoly (see D-Story-World-Economy.txt)
* December 2013 to early 2014, A.I.M. and the fake Mandarin: terror attacks on U.S. soil, so defense and security rise
* Spring 2014, S.H.I.E.L.D. and Hydra exposed: a government intelligence agency collapses in public. Contractors tied to it fall
* Spring 2015, Ultron destroys a Latverian city: a European country loses a city. Reconstruction and defense money follow
* Spring 2016, the Latveria Accords: registration law, which creates a compliance and insurance industry around superhumans
* Summer 2016, Wakanda opens to the world: the biggest shock of all, since a hidden nation with technology decades ahead joins the world economy. Long term it changes everything
* Spring 2018, the Snap: half of all life gone

The Snap Ends Normal Investing

* Half of every company's customers, employees, suppliers and shareholders vanish in an instant
* Half of every landlord's tenants and half of every lender's borrowers are gone. Property values and debt markets are meaningless for years
* Shares held by the vanished sit unclaimed. Nobody knows whether to treat them as dead or missing
* Supply chains break because half of every link is gone
* Any company whose value came from scale, crowds or growth is hit hardest
* Five years of shrinking population follow, from 2018 to 2023
* Fall 2023, the return: billions of people come back at once to a world that moved on without them. Housing, jobs and borders all have to absorb them
* No real-world data covers any of this. Everything after spring 2018 in this story is invented

What That Means for D's Foreknowledge

* His knowledge is good for about nineteen years, from September 1999 to spring 2018. Then it runs out
* He knows the Snap is coming, which is worth more than any stock pick. It is also the hardest thing to act on without explaining how he knows
* Selling everything before spring 2018 is the single most suspicious thing he could possibly do
* Hard assets, land and the business survive the Snap better than paper does. People still need to eat and still need things moved
* The courier company is the right shape for this world. It profits from every disaster, it needs no market to function and it does not depend on anyone's stock price

The Real Constraint for the Story

D's problem in September 1999 is not knowing what to buy:

* He is fourteen. A minor could not open a brokerage account alone in 1999. He needs a custodial account with an adult, which means telling an adult something
* He has no money. He lives in a children's home, so the first stake has to come from somewhere. That is a story problem worth playing rather than skipping
* Trading in 1999 means a phone call to a broker or a dial-up account, a commission on every trade and no instant information
* Explosive returns take decades. Monster's run is a twenty-year hold, not a quick score
* Sudden unexplained wealth draws attention from the home, the school, the IRS and anyone watching
* Picking only winners for twenty years looks like insider trading to anyone who checks

Story Uses

* A small, patient, boring portfolio explains the wealth D and Harry have by 2012 without anyone being able to prove anything
* Selling in early 2000 and buying back in late 2002 is a cleaner demonstration of foreknowledge than any single pick and the one a reader will recognize
* Monster Beverage is the perfect joke pick. A fortune made on canned energy drinks while everyone else chased the internet
* The 2008 crisis is where D can look like a genius and a villain at once, since he profits while the city suffers
* The money funds the courier company, the labs and Oscorp's turn toward medicine (see D-Story-World-Economy.txt)
* The Snap is the natural end of the investing thread. After 2018 D's wealth has to come from what he owns and what he can do, not from what he knows about the market
