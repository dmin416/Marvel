Part 1: Real Spider Baselines

Visual Acuity

* Jumping spider principal eyes resolve about 0.04 degrees (about 2.4 arcminutes), roughly two spots 0.12 mm apart at 20 cm
* Comparison: human acuity 0.007 degrees against 0.04 degrees for Portia, comparable to a domestic cat. Other sources give typical human acuity as about 1 arcminute (0.017 degrees)
* The principal eye works like a Galilean telescope (a long tube with lenses at both ends)
* The principal eye retina covers only about 0.6 degrees and holds no more than about 1,500 receptors
* Acuity comes from a small dense region. The secondary eyes cover motion across a wide field
* Primary source: Harland and Jackson, "Eight-legged cats and how they see" (2000). Portia acuity is 0.04 degrees (Williams and McIntyre 1980), which is 2.4 arcminutes
* Dragonfly acuity (highest known in insects) is 0.4 degrees, so Portia is ten times finer despite much smaller eyes
* Human acuity of 0.007 degrees (Land 1981) is only about five times better than Portia
* At 200 mm Portia can separate objects 0.12 mm apart
* The human retina has roughly 100 million photoreceptors (Curcio 1990 counted about 92 million rods and 4.6 million cones) while a salticid principal eye has only thousands
* The principal eyes cover a small field of about 1 to 2 degrees (Land 1969). Williams and McIntyre (1980) showed a pit at the rear of the tube acts as a lens and gives even better acuity than Land found

Danger Sense

* Wandering spider (Cupiennius salei) has about 100 trichobothria (fine air-flow hairs) on each leg
* Hair clusters hold 2 to 24 hairs of different lengths to widen the frequency range
* Modeled response range is about 10 to 950 Hz
* The spider detects the airflow of a flying blowfly while the fly is still about 38 mm away
* Behavioral range for a humming fly is about 20 cm in all horizontal directions. A suprathreshold response can occur at 50 to 70 cm
* Web builders also locate prey and threats from silk vibration
* Real spiders also sense atmospheric electric fields through the same kind of hairs (Erigone)

Organ and Circulatory Power

* Spider legs extend by hydraulic pressure and not by extensor muscles
* Parry and Brown (Journal of Experimental Biology, vol. 36, 1959) measured leg pressure in intact spiders. The journal PDF returned an access error twice. A secondary source reports about 6.6 kPa resting (about 50 mmHg) and transient pressure up to about 60 kPa (about 450 mmHg). The paper's own numbers were not reached
* A later study on Filistata hibernalis found resting leg pressure averaging about 5.1 kPa (about 38 mmHg)
* For comparison normal human systolic pressure is about 16 kPa (120 mmHg). 60 kPa is about 450 mmHg
* Hemolymph is a shear-thinning fluid (Goettler 2021, Soft Matter)
* Resting heart rate across 18 spider species ranged from 9 to 125 beats per minute (1976 laser study). It depends on body size and foraging energetics
* A wolf spider in situ averaged 145 beats per minute (1967 thesis). A redback spider measured about 75 beats per minute at rest (2025 study)
* Hearts of all studied spiders kept pumping during locomotion and maximum rate varied by species
* No peer-reviewed heart rate was found for jumping spiders or tarantulas
* No verified link between heart rate and jump force was found

Web Shooting and Silk Speed

* Spitting spider (Scytodes thoracica): glue and silk ejected at up to 28.8 m/s with a mean near 10.3 m/s
* The whole ejection lasts under about 30 to 35 ms
* The silk contracts 40 to 60% within 0.2 s of ejection and pulls with about 0.1 to 0.3 mN
* Normal silk spinning: Nephila silk is spun at about 2 to 20 mm/s. Silk spun outside this window is weaker
* Walking and frame draw speed: about 25 mm/s
* Forced reeling studies cover 0.1 to 400 mm/s with faster reeling raising stiffness and lowering stretch
* Spitting is the only fast silk projection with solid speed data

Strength

* Tangle-web spiders haul prey up to about 50 times their own weight using multiple silk lines in a pulley method (Greco and Pugno, Journal of the Royal Society Interface)
* Evarcha arcuata adhesion: safety factor about 160 (cited up to 173), a theoretical value
* Claims of spiders lifting 170 times their weight or 8x to 50x lack primary sources
* No direct bite force measurement in newtons exists in the sources found. Forum figures are speculation
* Black and colleagues (Evolution 2024) estimated bite force from micro-CT scans of chelicerae muscle. Size-corrected inferred force is lower in web builders than in free hunters

Explosive Power

* Splendid peacock spider acceleration: 127.8 m/s2 or about 13 g, more than twice the previous jumping spider record
* Phidippus princeps: takeoff about 0.83 m/s and about 5.24 g
* Regal jumping spider: up to about 6 body lengths per jump
* Trap-jaw spider (Zearchaea): mean jaw closure 0.084 ms with peak linear velocity 18.2 m/s
* Muscle direct power in animals is about 100 to 500 W per kg
* Catapult systems in insects reach about 160,000 W per kg. Spider jumps use hydraulics and no spider-specific figure was found

Part 2: Donor Traits

Octopus

* Camouflage: 10 to 20 million chromatophore organs plus about a million iridescent cells in the skin
* Color and pattern change in as little as about 200 ms (from a diving-safety source so treat as approximate)
* Cost: a fully active chromatophore system uses metabolic energy nearly equal to the resting metabolic rate (2024 study)
* Arm regeneration: new tips fully regenerated in about 130 days. First signs about 3 weeks. A 10-week figure exists for another species and injury type

Electric Eel

* Record discharge: 860 V from Electrophorus voltai (Nature Communications 2019). Previous record 650 V. Large specimens reach about 600 V
* Peak current about 1 A when short circuited. An estimate and not a precise measurement
* About 0.15 V per electrocyte (secondary review). Cells stack in series for voltage and in parallel for current
* About 2,000 to 5,000 stacked layers produce 300 to 800 V externally (same review). Textbooks say only several thousand
* Catania measured discharge repetition up to about 400 per second, a 2.5 ms period and about 0.5 ms half-width per peak (secondary report)
* A 2 ms duration for the full shock appears in one source but conflicts with the pulse measurements. Treat it as unverified
* 860 V x 1 A = 860 W is a nominal peak product and not delivered power
* Delivered power estimate: about 63 W (0.24 A) to a human trunk from a 1.8 m eel. An extrapolation from a 40 cm eel
* The eel is already human sized so its figures need no scaling

Axolotl

* Regrows limbs, spinal cord, brain tissue and heart tissue with no scar
* New neurons wire into surviving circuits
* Macrophages are required. Removing them blocks regeneration and causes scarring
* Juvenile axolotl limb regeneration takes about 40 to 50 days (University of Kentucky salamander resource)
* The standard staging system (Tank, Carlson and Connelly) was built on 10 to 12 month old animals with stages from wound healing through digit outgrowth
* The first 28 days cover amputation, early bud, late bud and pallet stages (Voss 2015)
* One logbook reports about 5 weeks of fast growth after the blastema forms and then about 9 more weeks of slow growth until the limb matches the other side (low quality source)
* A review says timing depends on size and age and older animals take longer. It also says regeneration completes within a month, which conflicts with the logbook
* No primary source gives a clean adult digit-complete day. A working range for an adult forelimb is about 1 to 4 months
* Adults of other Ambystoma species took about 155 to 375 days. Hobbyist reports range from days for toes to months for a limb

Planarian

* A fragment as small as 1/279 of the body can regrow a whole worm (Morgan)
* About 10,000 cells in that fragment (Montgomery and Coward 1974)
* Neoblasts are the only dividing cells and include pluripotent stem cells
* Neoblasts rebuild skin, gut, muscle and brain
* Head regeneration including a functional brain takes less than a week (Genome Biology 2011)
* Tail regeneration is partial at 6 to 8 days and complete at 10 to 13 days (protocol paper)

Tardigrade

* Median lethal radiation dose: about 5,000 Gy gamma and 6,200 Gy heavy ions when hydrated
* The figures above are hydrated Milnesium tardigradum (Horikawa 2006). Its LD50 was above 4,000 Gy in both the hydrated and dried states, with tests run up to 7,000 Gy gamma and 8,000 Gy heavy ions
* Richtersius coronifer: survival was unaffected at 500 and 1,000 Gy both hydrated and dried, with tests up to 5,000 Gy hydrated and 9,000 Gy dried (Jonsson 2005)
* A lethal dose for a human is about 5 to 10 Gy
* Temperature range about -272 C to about 149 C for short periods in the dried state
* Dsup protein cut DNA damage by about 40% in human cells in culture. A cell result and not whole-animal evidence
* The dried state cuts metabolism to a tiny fraction of normal (secondary source figure of 0.01%)
* FOTON-M3 mission (September 2007, Jonsson and colleagues, Current Biology 2008): dried tardigrades in a Biopan-6 module outside the capsule at about 270 km altitude
* Most survived open-space vacuum and cosmic radiation
* Some survived solar UV more than 1000 times the Earth surface level. One report gives a dose above 7000 kJ per square meter
* Survivors reproduced. This was a single mission with dried animals

Part 3: Chimera Design Values

All values below are proposals. Baselines are the real figures above.

* Visual acuity
  * Spider-style principal eyes at human scale with ogre-faced night sensitivity
  * Proposed: 3 to 5 times human acuity with a wide motion-sensing field and strong low light vision
* Danger sense
  * Trichobothria scaled to human size across the arms and legs plus electric-field sensing
  * Real spiders sense a fly at 38 mm and act at 20 cm. Proposed human-scale awareness out to about 10 to 20 m for fast incoming objects
* Organ power
  * Hydraulic assist for limb extension at peak pressure of about 60 kPa at spider scale
  * Proposed: a second hydraulic circuit alongside the heart peaking near 450 mmHg for explosive moves
  * Heart and lungs scaled for sustained output with fast recovery. Resting spider heart rates are low (9 to 125 bpm) so a human-scale rate is a free design choice
* Web shooting
  * Spitting spider is the speed reference at 28.8 m/s peak (about 104 km/h)
  * Proposed: web fired at 25 to 30 m/s with a draw line that spins out near normal silk speed and not faster, since fast silk is weaker
  * Silk toughness of Darwin's bark spider as the line quality target
* Strength
  * Prey hauling about 50 times body weight through pulley lines
  * Adhesion at the theoretical 160 times body weight, though real values are likely lower
  * Muscle strength is a free design choice because no spider strength record exists
* Explosive power
  * Peacock spider acceleration of about 13 g as the launch reference
  * Trap-jaw strike speed of 18.2 m/s as the burst-strike reference
  * Hydraulic launch at 6 body lengths gives about 10.5 m per jump
* Regeneration
  * Axolotl base: scar-free limb, spinal cord, brain and heart repair
  * Planarian stem cells: tiny fragments can rebuild larger structures
  * Planarian timing as the fast anchor: functional head and brain in under a week
  * Axolotl timing as the standard anchor: juvenile limb in about 40 to 50 days and an adult limb in roughly 1 to 4 months
  * Octopus arm timing as the slow anchor: first signs in weeks and full arm tip in about 130 days
  * Cost: heavy calorie burn and sleep during regrowth
* Camouflage
  * Octopus chromatophore skin changing in about 200 ms
  * Cost: energy use near resting metabolism while active
* Electric organ
  * Eel organ at human body scale produces up to about 600 to 860 V at about 1 A in pulses of about 0.5 ms half-width fired up to about 400 times per second
  * Proposed: pulses with a delivered shock to a target in the tens of watts range
  * Limit: repeated discharges need recovery
* Radiation and cold tolerance
  * Tardigrade-derived protection
  * Proposed: strong radiation resistance far above human lethal limits and long survival in extreme cold or vacuum in a dormant state
  * Real tardigrade tolerance depends on a dried dormant state, so the story should make this a shutdown mode
  * Orbital survival is documented for dried animals only (FOTON-M3, 2007)
