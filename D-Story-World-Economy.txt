D Story: World Economy and Power

Note: This is my own version of the story and its characters. Marvel and other works are inspirations only. References to canon, Raimi or other sources describe the inspiration and are not fixed facts of this story unless a file marks them as approved.

Status: planning ideas only. Nothing here is set unless it is marked Played. The only story canon is D-Story-So-Far.txt. Sources are the MCU timeline book, the Raimi and Webb films, the Netflix Daredevil series, the comics and Insomniac's Spider-Man game.

Accuracy note: the real-world 1999 economy and the list of conflicts were written from memory and not fact checked. Verify any real-world date or figure before relying on it.

Big Picture (Proposed)

* The world runs on the real 1999 economy with a Marvel layer on top. Most people live ordinary lives and never see a superhuman
* Money and power sit in four layers: governments, megacorporations, shadow organizations and city crime
* Defense money is the engine. Governments pay Stark, Hammer, Oscorp and others for weapons and enhancement research
* Advanced tech leaks downward. It moves from the companies to the shadow groups through theft and secret sales, then to street crime through armorers and salvage
* Crime money moves back up through legitimate fronts: construction, real estate, shipping and charities
* The police are underfunded and outmatched, comic style. That gap is what lets crime bosses and heroes both operate

1999: The Starting Economy (Proposed)

* The U.S. is the strongest economy and military in the world
* The dot-com boom is near its peak. Tech and defense stocks are climbing (Played: the Bugle stock box shows Oscorp and Stark Industries both rising)
* Y2K fears run through the fall of 1999 and peak at New Year's Eve
* The euro launched in January 1999 as a banking currency. Coins and notes come in 2002
* Oil is cheap at the start and rises over the next decade
* New York is recovering from the crime of the 1980s and early 1990s but the city in this story stays rougher than the real one
* Proposed later shocks from the real world: the dot-com crash from 2000 to 2002 and the financial crisis of 2008

Wars and Conflicts

Real-world conflicts around 1999 (proposed as background):

* Kosovo War: NATO bombing ended in June 1999. Peacekeepers are on the ground
* Second Chechen War: began in the fall of 1999
* East Timor: a UN-backed force deploys in September 1999
* Kargil War between India and Pakistan: May to July 1999
* Second Congo War: 1998 to 2003
* Eritrean-Ethiopian War: 1998 to 2000
* Sierra Leone Civil War: ongoing

Open questions:

* Whether this world follows the real attacks of September 11, 2001 and the wars that followed in Afghanistan and Iraq
* The MCU shows U.S. forces in Afghanistan in spring 2008, when Tony demonstrates the Jericho missile and is captured. Some U.S. war in Afghanistan has to exist by then

Marvel-layer conflicts:

* Wakanda stays hidden and never fights openly until 2016 to 2018
* The Ten Rings fund coups, insurgencies and mercenary wars around the world
* 2012: the Chitauri invasion of New York
* 2014: the Hydra uprising inside S.H.I.E.L.D.
* 2015: Ultron destroys a Latverian city
* 2016: the Avengers' civil war over the Latveria Accords
* 2018: Thanos' attack on Wakanda and the Snap

Governments and Nations

United States government

* What it does: buys weapons and enhancement research, regulates companies and runs the military
* Power: the largest defense budget in the world. It decides which company wins
* Played: Oscorp has an Army contract for a "human performance program"
* Military pressure on Oscorp for results is what pushes Norman toward testing the enhancer on himself

S.H.I.E.L.D.

* What it does: a secret intelligence and security agency answering to the World Security Council
* Power: global reach, hidden budgets, Stark technology and files on every unusual person
* Founded in 1946 by Peggy Carter and Howard Stark
* Hydra has hidden inside it since Arnim Zola joined after World War II
* In 1999 the public does not know it exists
* 2014: Hydra is exposed and S.H.I.E.L.D. collapses. Its weapons and agents scatter onto the street

Wakanda

* What it does: pretends to be a poor farming nation in Africa
* Power: the world's only large vibranium supply and technology decades ahead of everyone else
* Hidden from the global economy until it opens to the world in 2016
* 1992: Ulysses Klaue stole vibranium from Wakanda and sold it on the black market

Latveria

* What it does: a small European monarchy
* Power: little on the world stage. Its royal family has old money
* Victor Von Doom is a younger son of the royal family who runs Von Doom Industries in New York (see D-Story-Fantastic-Four.txt)
* Sokovia does not exist. Latveria takes its place in every MCU event

Megacorporations

Stark Industries

* What it does: the largest weapons maker in the United States. Missiles, munitions and defense systems for the U.S. and its allies
* Power: government contracts, the best engineers and a public face in Tony Stark
* Founded by Howard Stark. Tony took over after Howard and Maria died in 1991. Obadiah Stane runs much of the business
* Played: Stark Industries has a sign on a New York building and has unveiled a new missile line
* Hidden: Stane secretly sells Stark weapons to the Ten Rings and other buyers
* 2008: Tony is captured in Afghanistan, builds the first Iron Man suit and shuts down weapons manufacturing. The stock falls
* After 2008 Stark moves into clean energy with the arc reactor
* 2012: Stark Tower in Manhattan becomes Avengers Tower
* After 2012 Stark and the U.S. government form Damage Control

Roxxon

* What it does: oil, gas and energy across the world
* Power: fuel supply, pipelines and lobbying
* Shows up in the MCU on gas station signs and tankers. In the comics it is a ruthless corporation that hires villains
* Idea: a big courier customer and a quiet source of corporate crime

Major Corporations

Oscorp

* What it does: began in chemicals and is moving into military biotechnology
* Power: New York's biggest homegrown science company, Army money and a research campus along the river
* Played: the Army contract for a "human performance program." Research head Dr. Mendel Stromm. Oscorp is approaching university labs including Empire State's
* Programs: performance enhancement and genetic engineering. The super spiders are not Oscorp's. They come from Columbia's own spider lab
* Norman Osborn is under pressure from the military and the board. The board can push him out
* Later: Norman's madness, the board's deaths and the takeover. Harry runs it after. Idea: D and Peter lead its deep science division
* Insomniac inspiration: Devil's Breath, an Oscorp bioweapon

Columbia University Spider Lab

* Its own thing, separate from Oscorp. A university genetics laboratory, not a defense contractor
* Cindy Moon runs it
* It engineers the super spiders. Gwen is bitten there on the senior trip, spring or summer 2003. D collects the spider
* Funding is university money, grants and philanthropy, not an Army contract. Estimated multiyear cost of the genetics program: $10M to $75M, which is small next to Oscorp's programs
* Oscorp approaches university labs, including Empire State's (Played). Whether it ever reaches for Columbia's spider work is open
* Cindy later makes the neutralizer that takes Gwen's powers away permanently

Oscorp Finances (fictional model, about 2003)

All of these are storytelling figures. The films never disclose Oscorp's accounts.

* Annual revenue: $3B
* Annual research and development: $450M
* Equity value: $5B
* Interest-bearing debt: $1.5B
* Cash and short-term investments: $120M
* The threatened Army program: about $900M of contracted work left over three years, roughly $300M a year
* The shape of it: rich in prototypes and patents, poor in cash. Norman is desperate because investors will not fund another long research cycle
* Why one lost contract nearly kills it: contract concentration, debt that has to be refinanced and a board that will sell rather than ride out another cycle. Losing $900M of contract face value is not losing $900M of cash, so the crisis is about financing and confidence
* Stress test: $120M of cash against $75M a month of net outflow in a crisis gives about 1.6 months of runway with no credit facility

Norman's Personal Money (fictional model, about 2003)

Money held by Oscorp is not Norman's. Owning a third of the stock does not let him withdraw a third of the company's cash.

* Oscorp shares, 35% of $5B: $1.75B on paper, concentrated and illiquid
* Bank deposits and short-term Treasuries: $25M, the part he can actually spend
* Other public securities: $110M, sellable with tax and market friction
* Private and venture stakes: $40M, slow to sell
* Real estate: $35M. Art, cars and collectibles: $10M
* Debts: $30M
* Net worth: about $1.94B, with only about $25M near cash
* On his death the shares go into his estate. Harry running Oscorp does not mean Harry owns a majority of it. How the estate is settled is open

Quest Aerospace

* What it does: a defense contractor competing with Oscorp for the Army's performance program with a powered exoskeleton instead of a drug
* Fictional baseline: $1B to $4B annual revenue, $150M to $600M annual R&D, $2B to $8B equity value
* General Slocum gives Oscorp roughly two weeks to show a viable human trial or the money goes to Quest. That ultimatum is what pushes Norman to test the enhancer on himself
* After the Goblin sabotages Quest's demonstration, Oscorp overtakes Quest as the military's principal supplier
* Quest then makes a tender offer for Oscorp. The board moves to sell and to remove Norman, which is what sets off the World Unity Festival killings
* Open: what happens to Quest afterward and whether it comes back as a rival to Harry's Oscorp

Program Costs (fictional multiyear R&D, 2003 to 2005 dollars)

Do not add the maxima together. Programs share labs, staff and budgets.

* Oscorp nanotechnology platform: $50M to $250M
* Oscorp glider, flight controls and suit: $150M to $600M
* Oscorp human enhancement and biotech: $100M to $500M
* Goblin weapons subsystems: $20M to $120M, partly overlapping the glider work
* Quest powered exoskeleton: $100M to $500M
* Columbia super-spider genetics: $10M to $75M
* Octavius fusion reactor and robotic arms: $100M to $500M, funded by Harry's Oscorp in about 2005

Real Scale Checks

* FY2003 US defense research, development, test and evaluation: about $57B. DARPA: about $2.69B
* Joint Strike Fighter: a projected $33B development and demonstration phase over about ten years, per a 2003 GAO report
* Human Genome Project: about $2.7B across 1990 to 2003, in 1991 dollars
* So a $100M to $500M prototype program is substantial but ordinary inside a real defense research system. A single genetics lab is nowhere near national-program scale

Hammer Industries

* What it does: a second-tier defense contractor that always wants Stark's business
* Power: government contacts and Justin Hammer's salesmanship. Its technology is always behind Stark's
* 1999: smaller and hungry
* After 2008: Hammer bids for the contracts Stark abandons and tries to copy the Iron Man suit
* Spring 2010: Hammer's drones built with Ivan Vanko are hijacked at the Stark Expo. Justin Hammer is arrested

Von Doom Industries

* What it does: aerospace and engineering
* Power: Victor's family money and his ambition
* Played: buying American aerospace suppliers and rumored to be planning a private orbital platform
* Later: funds Reed Richards' space station mission. The cosmic storm and the failed mission wreck the company (see D-Story-Fantastic-Four.txt)

Pym Technologies

* What it does: Hank Pym's company, built on shrinking research he keeps hidden
* Power: science that S.H.I.E.L.D. wanted and never got
* Hank quit S.H.I.E.L.D. in 1989. Darren Cross and Hope van Dyne later push him out of the company
* 2015: Cross tries to sell the Yellowjacket suit to Hydra

Damage Control

* What it does: cleans up superhuman disasters. A joint venture of Stark and the U.S. government formed after the Battle of New York
* Power: federal authority over every scrap of alien tech
* Approved idea: D's company wins the Chitauri salvage hauling contract from Damage Control (or Oscorp). Adrian Toomes loses it and becomes the Vulture

Shadow Organizations

The Ten Rings

* What it does: a criminal empire that has run for about a thousand years under Xu Wenwu
* The rings: this story uses the MCU version, not the comics version
  * MCU: ten metal bands worn on the forearms, five per arm, that channel energy. Wenwu fights with them, extending and striking and blasting. They give him long life. Their origin is unknown even to him
  * Comics: the Mandarin wears ten separate finger rings, each with its own magic or alien power, derived from Makluan dragon technology. This story does not use that
* What that means: the rings are a weapon Wenwu wields through martial mastery, not a set of ten enchanted objects with ten separate powers. Nobody can pick up one ring and gain one ability
* Power: long life and great strength for Wenwu. The organization has armies, money and agents everywhere. It has toppled governments
* Business: weapons, mercenaries, coups and smuggling
* 2008: a Ten Rings cell under Raza holds Tony Stark in Afghanistan and buys Stark weapons through Stane
* 2013: Aldrich Killian borrows the name "the Mandarin" for a fake terrorist, which angers Wenwu
* Its reach is global. It rarely works in New York openly
* Traditionalism: Wenwu has had a thousand years to industrialize and has not. The organization runs on mastery, hierarchy, loyalty and personal power. It buys modern weapons rather than inventing them. That is why a millennium-old empire holds no technological edge over a seventy-year-old American company

Hydra

* What it does: a secret fascist organization hidden inside S.H.I.E.L.D. and governments
* Power: agents in high places, Winter Soldier assassinations and stolen research
* 1991: the Winter Soldier killed Howard and Maria Stark for super-soldier serum
* 2014: exposed by Project Insight. Its weapons end up on the street
* 2015: Baron Strucker runs experiments in Latveria with Loki's scepter

A.I.M. (Advanced Idea Mechanics)

* What it does: Aldrich Killian's think tank for dangerous research
* Power: scientists and military buyers
* New Year's Eve 1999: Killian tries to recruit Maya Hansen and Tony Stark in Bern. This falls inside the story's first months
* Extremis becomes its main product. Iron Man 3 runs from December 2013 to early 2014 (see D-Story-AIM.txt)

City Crime in New York

Kingpin (Wilson Fisk)

* What he does: runs organized crime in Hell's Kitchen
* Power: money, corrupt police, politicians, judges and the press. He hides behind legitimate businesses
* Business: construction, real estate, shipping, protection rackets, drugs and guns
* Stays in Hell's Kitchen with Daredevil and the Punisher
* After 2012 the rebuilding of Hell's Kitchen after the invasion becomes his biggest money source, as in the Netflix series
* Idea: the most important crime customer for a neutral courier

The Maggia

* What it does: the old crime families of New York
* Power: tradition, numbers and rackets across Manhattan
* Bosses in the comics: Silvermane and Hammerhead
* Comics: an experimental Maggia drug gave Cloak and Dagger their powers and in some versions Mister Negative's
* Competes with Fisk for anything outside Hell's Kitchen

Mister Negative (Martin Li)

* What he does: in 1999 Martin Li is a genuine philanthropist who runs F.E.A.S.T. in Chinatown
* Played: a Bugle article quotes Li about the F.E.A.S.T. community meals
* Later: Mister Negative is the crime lord side of him. He leads the Inner Demons gang and uses F.E.A.S.T. as a front
* Power: Darkforce blasts, great strength and a touch that corrupts people into loyal servants
* Grudge: in the Insomniac version Norman's experiment killed Li's parents and gave Li his powers. That gives him a reason to target Oscorp and later Harry
* Timing in the story: the major Mister Negative arc is planned for 2012 to 2014

Tombstone (Lonnie Lincoln)

* What he does: Harlem crime boss
* Power: muscle, money and in the comics near-unbreakable skin
* 2010: Hulk fights Abomination in Harlem close to his turf. General Ross waged a war in the neighborhood to catch Banner, which is why this fight could not stay quiet

Street Level

* Armed robbery crews hit stores and jewelers. Played: a crew robbed Midtown jewelers in September 1999. One arrest was made and two of the crew are still out
* The Tinkerer (Phineas Mason) builds and repairs weapons for other criminals
* The Big Man and the Enforcers work rackets in Manhattan

Media

Technology Level: Advanced and Not Advanced

The approved shape

* This world is far ahead of the real 1999 in a few narrow places and exactly level with it everywhere else
* The reason is who holds the advances. A handful of companies and one secret agency hold them. None of them share

Where the world is ahead of the real one

* Oscorp: military biotechnology, genetic engineering and performance enhancement well past anything real. The super spiders exist. The enhancer exists. Regeneration research is funded
* Stark Industries: the arc reactor, weapons guidance and materials decades ahead. Howard built a working flying car in the 1940s
* Pym Technologies: shrinking matter, which is physics nobody else has. Hank quit S.H.I.E.L.D. in 1989 rather than share it and has hidden it since
* S.H.I.E.L.D.: it holds the Tesseract, Hydra's wartime energy weapons, a Kree body and the files on everything unusual. None of it reaches the public
* Wakanda: decades ahead of everyone, hidden behind a farming nation's disguise until 2016
* Von Doom Industries: aerospace and a private orbital platform before any government or company in the real world had one
* Smaller pockets: Curt Connors on limb regeneration, Otto Octavius on fusion, A.I.M. on Extremis from 1999, Dr. Noah Burstein on super-soldier regeneration at Seagate

Where the world is exactly the real 1999

* Consumer technology: dial-up internet, Pentium III computers, pagers and early mobile phones, no smartphones, no social media
* Medicine for ordinary people: hospitals, drugs and surgery as they really were
* Cars, planes, trains and buildings
* Energy: oil, gas and coal. The arc reactor is one company's secret and powers nothing public
* The general economy: the real 1999 dot-com boom, the real markets, the real wages

Why the gap stays open

* Secrecy: every advance sits inside a company, a nation or an agency that profits from keeping it
* Military customers: the Army pays Oscorp and Stark for exclusivity, not for publication
* No academic pipeline: the breakthroughs happen in private labs, not universities, so nothing is published and nothing is built on
* Hidden holders: Wakanda hides on purpose, Pym hides on purpose, S.H.I.E.L.D. hides by charter
* The Ten Rings: a thousand-year empire with no interest in industrializing (see above). Its power is personal mastery, so it adds nothing to the world's technology and takes nothing from it
* Traditionalism generally: the oldest powers in this world, Wenwu's organization and the Maggia families among them, run on hierarchy and loyalty rather than research. Age does not mean advancement

What that means for the story

* An ordinary person in 1999 New York lives in the real 1999. D included
* The advanced world is a set of locked rooms. Getting inside one is the whole point of a courier business
* Nobody can build on anybody else's work, so progress is lumpy and jumps forward only when something leaks or escapes
* The leaks are the plot: Norman's enhancer, the escaped spiders, Chitauri salvage, S.H.I.E.L.D.'s collapse in 2014 and Oscorp's own projects
* Stark going public with the Iron Man suit in 2008 is the first time any of it becomes visible, which is why that year changes everything
* D's symbiote is the extreme case. It can do more than any of these companies and sits inside one person who tells nobody

How It All Connects

* Government money pays the companies. The companies compete for contracts and cut corners to win them
* The companies leak technology. Stane sells to the Ten Rings, Hammer copies and steals, Oscorp's experiments escape and A.I.M. sells to the highest bidder
* Shadow groups turn the leaked tech into private armies and coups
* City bosses buy what reaches New York and launder their money through construction, real estate and charities
* Every disaster creates a cleanup and rebuilding boom: Damage Control after 2012, Fisk's Hell's Kitchen projects and salvage crews like Toomes'
* Heroes rise where the police can't cope. The government answers with registration in the 2016 Latveria Accords

Global Shocks Over the Story (MCU Timeline Book)

* Spring 2008: Stark leaves weapons and the defense market scrambles. Hammer pushes in
* Spring 2010: the Stark Expo drone disaster. Hulk and Abomination wreck Harlem. General Ross waged a war in the neighborhood to catch Banner, which is why this fight could not stay quiet, so it is the outlier against the Hell's Kitchen circle of silence
* Spring 2012: the Chitauri invasion of New York. Huge damage and a rebuilding boom. Alien tech reaches the black market
* December 2013 to early 2014: the fake Mandarin attacks and A.I.M.'s Extremis
* Spring 2014: S.H.I.E.L.D. falls. Weapons scatter and intelligence agencies reshuffle
* Spring 2015: Ultron destroys a Latverian city and kills its rulers
* Spring 2016: the Latveria Accords. Wakanda opens to the world later in 2016
* Spring 2018: the Snap erases half of all life. Markets crash, jobs and homes go empty and supply chains break
* 2018 to 2023: five years of shrinking population and recovery
* Fall 2023: the vanished return. Housing, jobs and borders all have to absorb billions of people at once

Courier Angle (Ideas)

* D's neutral courier business can serve every layer: Stark and Oscorp labs, Hammer's offices, Von Doom Industries and the Baxter Building, Fisk's fronts and F.E.A.S.T.
* Every delivery puts D inside a lab, warehouse or penthouse and lets him see where the money moves
* Damage Control's salvage contract is the business's biggest break

Mutants

Oscorp's Ideological Reformation (Approved)

* D serves as a kind of financial advisor to Harry and shares in the wealth. He earns that share through his knowledge and through his beliefs
* Oscorp has an enormous amount of arms wealth. D convinces Harry to turn the company toward medicine and helping people
* The pitch: medicine and charity pay better over a lifetime than weapons do. They also build the thing the weapons money cannot buy
* What they build:
  * Medicine: Oscorp's research money moves from military biotechnology toward treatment, vaccines, prosthetics and regeneration. Harry's own genetic curse gives him a personal reason
  * Small loans: credit for people banks will not touch, in the neighborhoods they own property in
  * Housing: the buildings they took after 2012 become places people can afford to live rather than lots to flip
  * Jobs: the courier company, the salvage work, construction and the labs all hire locally
  * Donations: a steady flow of charitable money, which brings more donations in from others
* Why it works economically: loans, housing and jobs put money into circulation instead of taking it out. The neighborhood grows, which raises the value of everything they own. Doing good turns out to be the better business
* Why it works for them: Oscorp's name changes from the company that makes weapons for the Army to the company that fixes people. That buys goodwill, political cover and the public's benefit of the doubt
* The contradiction: they go on selling weapons the whole time. D is a Christian and knows better. Harry inherited an arms company and is not giving up its revenue
  * Nobody in the group makes an issue of it. They are being good Christian people with one hand and filling Army contracts with the other
  * The contradiction is deliberate and stays unresolved unless the story chooses to resolve it
  * It is also the thing an enemy, a reporter or a friend can throw in their faces at the worst moment
* Contrast with Fisk: Fisk takes money out of Hell's Kitchen and D and Harry put it in. That is why the neighborhood eventually sides with them and why Fisk cannot buy the loyalty he had in the show
* Contrast with Stark: Tony quits weapons publicly in 2008 after Afghanistan and loses the stock price for it. Oscorp pivots quietly and keeps the revenue
* Open: whether anyone inside Oscorp fights the pivot, such as the board or the military customers
* Open: whether D ever faces the contradiction directly
* Open: how much of D's advice comes from unlocked knowledge and how much from faith

The 2012 Rebuilding Contracts (Approved)

* By the Battle of New York in May 2012, D and Harry have enough wealth between them to take an enormous slice of what would have been Fisk's monopoly
* D brings his courier company and the Chitauri salvage hauling contract. Harry brings Oscorp's money, name and labs
* Where the money is: insurance payouts, federal disaster money, demolition and hauling, construction and the damaged property that sells cheap right after the invasion
* Fisk's plan in the Netflix series was to buy the damaged property cheap, launder money through construction shells and push tenants out. In this story most of that is already taken
* What D and Harry take: the legitimate contracts, the big hauling and salvage work and the buildings Fisk wanted. They can pay market price and show clean books, which Fisk cannot match in a public bid
* What Fisk keeps: the rackets, the drugs and guns, the bribed officials and whatever he can take by force or fraud. He is still dangerous and still runs Hell's Kitchen's underworld
* Consequences:
  * Fisk now has a reason to come after D and Harry. He loses the fortune that made him untouchable in the show
  * Tenants who would have been pushed out stay. That changes the neighborhood and whose side the people are on
  * D and Harry are suddenly visible as major property owners, which is a problem for people who want to stay quiet
  * The money funds the group's hero work and labs without needing anyone's permission
* Open: whether D and Harry bid openly or through holding companies
* Open: how Fisk answers and whether he goes after the companies, the people or the buildings
* Open: whether the Defenders see D and Harry as allies or as another kind of landlord

The Defenders and Hell's Kitchen (Approved)

* Fisk runs Hell's Kitchen. Matt Murdock, Frank Castle, Jessica Jones, Luke Cage and Danny Rand handle it themselves
* Nobody snitches. As in any neighborhood, the people there do not talk to outsiders
* So the whole Hell's Kitchen situation stays under Hydra's and S.H.I.E.L.D.'s radar
* What that means:
  * The street-level heroes are a local secret, not a federal case file
  * S.H.I.E.L.D. and Hydra have no useful intelligence on them, so neither recruits them, hunts them or counts them in their plans
  * After S.H.I.E.L.D. falls in 2014 there are no files on them to leak
  * The Latveria Accords in 2016 cannot cover people the government cannot name
  * Fisk benefits too. The same silence that protects the heroes protects him
* Story use for D: a neutral courier is trusted exactly because he does not talk. That is the price of entry in Hell's Kitchen and the reason he can carry for Fisk and the heroes both
* Open: whether D is inside the circle of silence or just another outsider who gets nothing
* Outlier: the 2010 Hulk and Abomination fight in Harlem. Ross brought the Army into the neighborhood to catch Banner, so that one played out in public and in the papers no matter who talked
