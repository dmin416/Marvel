# Cost of goods, work and living — comparative economic reference

> **Purpose:** Support D / NovaCorp worldbuilding when deciding **what goods actually cost to produce, what workers should be paid, and what households can afford**. This is an *external reference and calculation worksheet*, **not** a second financial ledger, a new NovaCorp wage policy, or an enacted story event. The single policy and financial authority remains `d_finances_taxes_and_company_control.md` (the **NovaCorp Pay Standard**). The source data below were **supplied by the user**, not independently verified, and are retained for later investigation and correction. Do not promote individual estimates into factual historical prices, completed NovaCorp transactions or legally required wage rates without checking the relevant year, region, currency, law and vendor.

## How to use this reference in D's world

**Three separate questions must be answered rather than collapsed into a single dollar amount:**

1. **Production cost:** What inputs must be paid for to make and deliver one good or hour of service? Count materials, purchased components, internal labor, employer-side benefits and payroll burdens, energy, tooling and machinery depreciation, facilities, engineering and compliance, yield/scrap, freight, selling/distribution, repairs, warranty, relevant taxes, financing and necessary reserves. Where suppliers already charge for upstream components, **do not add their embedded labor and material costs again**.
2. **Fair compensation:** Determine lawful cash wages, applicable overtime/leave/benefits, occupational skill, risk and responsibility, local housing/food/transport/medical costs, dependents, worker bargaining and credible outside-market alternatives. **A production-cost estimate is not by itself a justified wage**, and a household's current poverty spending is **not a fair-living benchmark**. Compare a worker's *spendable after-tax income* with essential costs, not the employer's fully loaded labor cost. Under NovaCorp's intended credit-pay policy, calculate cash wages, NC issuance, cash-redemption liability and actual purchasing power separately; adoption remains a future story decision.
3. **Affordable retail price:** Start from fully accounted unit costs and compliant taxes, then compare the offered price with households' available budgets and alternatives. If NovaCorp chooses to sell below cost, explicitly record the subsidy or cross-subsidy, its funding and its long-run feasibility. A cheap price does not prove efficient production; a high market price does not prove high physical resource cost.

### Working calculations — before declaring any price or wage

| Measure | Practical method | Essential safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| Fully loaded direct labor per unit | Paid production hours per good × employer cost per hour | Include paid leave/benefits in employer cost; do not use *billed tradesperson rates* as ordinary employee pay |
| Fully delivered unit cost | Purchased goods + attributable direct labor + energy + consumables + capex/depreciation + facility + freight + warranty + overhead + lawful product taxes | Do not count supplier value-add twice; distinguish fixed costs from variable costs and expected production volume |
| Cost per useful year | Upfront purchase + financing + energy + maintenance + replacements − residual value, divided by service years | Useful life and actual utilization differ across countries, customers and technologies |
| Worker annual gross-pay requirement | Solve for gross cash-equivalent compensation sufficient to cover taxes, an agreed household essential basket, savings and contingencies | Use real local taxes, paid hours, dependents and unavoidable noncash restrictions; never assume identical household size |
| Real purchasing power | After-tax pay ÷ local basket cost, or hours of work needed to buy a standardized basket | Specify currency, year, quality, household size, and what is publicly funded |
| NC affordability test | Published cash wage + redeemable credits, against *actual* prices, taxes and NC cash-out terms | The project's 2026 $25/NC example is **not** an automatic 2004 rate or an exchange rate for all countries |

To price D's 2004 NovaPhone or NovaCorp jobs, obtain or explicitly model **2004** materials, electronics components, labor, carrier plans, taxes, FX, production volumes and households in each target country. ### Evidence and modeling warnings

- The supplied 2005/2015/2026 numeric tables, currency conversions, exchange rates, 2026 projections, GDP and wages, lifecycle totals and embedded-resource estimates are **unverified source estimates**, sometimes mixing actual market prices, modeling conventions, social costs and speculative future comparisons. Preserve them as hypotheses until backed by date- and location-specific documentation.
- The supplied **2005→2026 ×1.39** and **2015→2026 ×1.32** inflation multipliers should **not** be used as authoritative general USD CPI deflators (US CPI-U rose roughly 1.6–1.7× from 2005 to 2026 and about 1.4× from 2015 to 2026, so ×1.39 understates the 2005 case) without independently verifying the price index and dates. Currency FX changes, inflation, local price levels and purchasing-power parity are distinct.
- The labor table mixes **gross pay**, **fully loaded employer labor cost** and **billed contractor rates**. Those figures cannot be compared as if they were all employee wages. Inputs described as “earth value” or imputed damage must not be treated as what a supplier invoices.
- Daily, annual and lifetime layers can overlap. **Do not add private spend, public allocations, infrastructure replacement, unpaid household work, or environmental damages** without checking which values are already represented in other categories. Long lifetime projections are conditional snapshots, not prices paid during an actual birth-to-death cohort.
- An international spread between Luxembourg and Southern Sudan / South Sudan describes **unequal access to money, infrastructure and public services**, not lower human value or a rationale for paying workers less than a decent locally viable standard.
- No source data here record **NovaCorp's own unit bills, payroll, actual product margins or employee living standards**. Those must be established separately in story or in the financial ledger before they are booked.
