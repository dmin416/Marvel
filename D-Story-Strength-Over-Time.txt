D Story: Strength Over Time

Note: This is my own version of the story and its characters. Marvel and other works are inspirations only. References to canon, Raimi or other sources describe the inspiration and are not fixed facts of this story unless a file marks them as approved.

Status: design brainstorm only. Nothing here is approved. The curve is a design estimate anchored to the sourced points listed below.

Premise

* D is an undernourished 14 year old orphan who is small for his age
* Strength is measured as a one rep floor lift (deadlift) in kilograms
* Two tracks: untrained and trained from age 18 (college)
* D starts training at 14 and it is already happening in the played story (yard workout). D-Story-Monthly-Strength.txt replaces the trained track below with a month-by-month model from 14 (168 kg at 22 and a 180 kg peak). The trained-from-18 column is kept only for comparison
* D trains to survive the world. Peter stays a weak nerd and Gwen takes self defense without weights and Harry trains just enough to attract women
* The old trained-from-18 track reached 140 kg at age 22

Sourced Anchors

* Peak strength velocity in boys falls near peak height velocity with a lag of about 0 to 12 months depending on the test (several adolescent studies). Early maturers are stronger than late maturers
* Grip strength peaks at about 51 kg in men aged 29 to 39 (Dodds 2014, PLoS ONE, 12 British studies). It holds through midlife and then declines
* Powerlifting performance peaks at about 27.7 to 31.5 years in one study. A larger dataset puts raw male lifters at 28 to 35 with the highest scores around 30 to 32. The decline after that is partly a selection effect
* Men who kept competing in powerlifting from before 40 to after 60 lost about 0.33% of their deadlift per year (Kinesiology, "Age Is a Poor Predictor of Maximal Strength in Competitive Powerlifters")
* A natural male beginner gains about 20 to 25 lb (9 to 11 kg) of muscle in year one of proper training and nutrition and about 10 to 12 lb, 5 to 6 lb and 2 to 3 lb in years two to four (Lyle McDonald model, sources disagree on size)
* Untrained adult man deadlift is about 70 kg. Novice men reach about 1.25 to 1.5 times body mass
* Catch-up in adult height is debated. Childhood stunting is not significantly made up in adolescence (review)
* No study was found measuring adult muscle strength after adolescent undernutrition. The effect on final strength is a design assumption

Phases

* 14 to 15: underweight and undernourished. Strength is far below normal for age
* 15 to 17: nutrition improves and strength climbs fastest
* 17 to 18: peak strength velocity. Steady food drives a large catch-up
* 18 to 22: college training. Novice gains dominate
* 22 to 28: intermediate gains slow as the body approaches its ceiling
* 28 to 32: peak
* 32 to 35: slow decline begins

Peak

* Untrained: about 97 kg at ages 28 to 31
* Trained from 18: about 164 kg at ages 30 to 31
* Trained D passes 90 percent of peak by age 24 and 95 percent by age 26
* Largest yearly gains on the trained track: +25 kg from 18 to 19 and +20 kg from 19 to 20
* Largest yearly gains on the untrained track: +15 kg per year from 16 to 18
* Decline from the peak to 35 is about 3.7 percent on the trained track. This is a design choice. The sourced decline of 0.33 percent per year comes from lifters who kept competing from about 40 into their 60s

Effect of Undernutrition

* A strong catch-up in the late teens lets him reach the same adult range
* Assumed permanent cost: slightly lower adult height than genetics predicted and a slightly lower strength ceiling (about 5 percent)
* The cost applies to the ceiling and not to how fast he trains once fed

Use With the Venom Model

* The venom multiplies the baseline at the age of the dose
* A dose at 22 multiplies 168 kg (D-Story-Monthly-Strength.txt)
* A dose at 26 to 31 would multiply the peak of about 180 kg
* Waiting to peak age improves the result by about 7 percent over a dose at 22
