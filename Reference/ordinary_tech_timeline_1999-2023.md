# Ordinary Technology Timeline: 1999 to 2023

The everyday technology ordinary people have in each year of the story: computers and the internet, phones, cameras, cars, television and news, money and payments, medicine and labs plus what a teenager with no money can get hold of. It complements the two superhero tech files (`mcu_alt_tech_timeline_1943-2008.md` and `mcu_tech_timeline_2008-2023.md`).

Accuracy note: written from memory and not fact checked. Release dates of well-known products are reliable. Adoption figures (how many people owned something) are approximate and marked as such. Verify any figure before the story depends on it.

---

## This World Versus Ours

- **Baseline:** ordinary consumer technology matches our world year for year. Phones, computers, cars and television are the same products at the same prices
- **Ahead of ours:** weapons, energy, biotech and aerospace inside a few companies. Stark Industries sells advanced missiles in 1999. Oscorp is moving from chemicals into military biotech. Von Doom Industries buys aerospace suppliers. Wakanda is decades ahead and hidden
- **How the gap shows up:** the advanced tech stays inside labs, militaries and black markets. Ordinary people see it on the news, in Stark Expo coverage and in crime scenes, not in their homes
- **When it starts leaking:**
  - 2008 onward: the arc reactor becomes public knowledge and Stark moves toward clean energy
  - 2012 onward: Chitauri salvage reaches street crime and Damage Control runs the cleanup
  - 2014 onward: Hydra weapons leak after S.H.I.E.L.D. collapses
  - 2016 onward: the Sokovia Accords put registration and tracking on enhanced people
- **2018 to 2023 (the Blip):** half of all life vanishes. Consumer tech largely stalls for five years. Real-world events of 2020 to 2022 (the COVID-19 pandemic, the mRNA vaccine rollout) are not shown in the MCU and should be treated as not happening unless the story decides otherwise

## 1999

- **Computers:** beige desktop towers running Windows 98. The colorful iMac G3 (1998) is in some homes and schools. Floppy disks and Zip disks hold files. CD burners are becoming common
- **Internet:** dial-up at up to 56k. The phone line is busy while someone is online. AOL CDs arrive in the mail. Google exists (founded 1998) but many people still use Yahoo, AltaVista and Ask Jeeves. AIM and ICQ for chat. Napster launches in June. Roughly a third to a half of U.S. adults are online (approximate)
- **Phones:** home landlines with cordless handsets and answering machines. Pay phones on every block in New York. Cell phones are common among adults with money but rare for teenagers (very roughly a third of Americans have one, approximate). Calls cost by the minute. Texting barely exists in the U.S. Pagers are still common
- **Cameras:** 35mm film and disposable cameras. Photos are developed at a drugstore in about an hour to a few days. Digital cameras exist at 1 to 2 megapixels and cost hundreds of dollars
- **Video and music:** VHS dominates. DVD players are spreading (launched 1997). TiVo launches in March. Walkman cassette players and Discman CD players. Early MP3 players exist and hold very few songs
- **Games:** PlayStation, Nintendo 64, Game Boy Color. Sega Dreamcast launches in September. Arcades still exist
- **Television and news:** cable TV with CNN, Fox News and MSNBC. Newspapers are the main local news source. The Daily Bugle sells on every newsstand
- **Money:** cash, checks and magnetic stripe credit cards. ATMs everywhere. No chip cards. Online shopping exists (Amazon, eBay) but most people do not use it. New York subway riders use MetroCards or tokens
- **Cars:** no built-in screens. Paper maps and printed MapQuest directions. GPS navigation is a luxury option
- **Medicine and labs:** PCR and DNA testing are standard in labs and forensics. The Human Genome Project is in progress. MRI and CT scanners in hospitals
- **Libraries and school:** card catalogs being replaced by a few online catalog computers. Encyclopedias on CD-ROM (Encarta). Typewriters still in some offices. Overhead projectors in classrooms
- **Y2K:** fear of computer failures at midnight on New Year's Eve peaks in the fall
- **Teen with no money:** library computers with time limits, pay phones with quarters, a used Walkman, borrowed VHS tapes, a disposable camera, a library card

## 2000

- **Y2K passes** with few real problems
- **Dot-com crash** begins in March
- **PlayStation 2** launches in the U.S. in October and also plays DVDs
- **USB flash drives** appear with tiny capacities
- **GPS** becomes accurate for civilians after the U.S. turns off deliberate signal degradation in May
- **Phones:** prepaid cell phones make phones possible for people without credit. Snake on Nokia phones

## 2001

- **iPod** launches in October (Mac only at first)
- **Windows XP** launches in October
- **Wikipedia** launches in January
- **Xbox and GameCube** launch in November
- **Napster** is shut down by court order
- **Broadband:** cable and DSL internet spread but dial-up is still common
- **September 11 attacks:** happen as in our world. No hero stops them. Afterward the Patriot Act (October) expands surveillance and the TSA (created November) takes over airport screening with much tighter security

## 2002

- **Camera phones** reach the U.S. late in the year with very low resolution
- **BlackBerry** devices add phone calls and become the business standard
- **Friendster** is an early social network
- **Euro** coins and notes enter circulation in January

## 2003 (college begins)

- **New York subway tokens** are retired. MetroCard only
- **311** non-emergency city service line launches in New York
- **iTunes Store** opens in April with songs at 99 cents
- **MySpace** launches
- **Skype** launches for free internet calls
- **Wi-Fi** spreads through laptops and coffee shops
- **Human Genome Project** is declared complete in April
- **Northeast blackout** on August 14 takes power from New York and much of the region
- **College life:** laptops become common. Campus Ethernet and dorm Wi-Fi. Text messaging grows. Flip phones

## 2004

- **Facebook** launches at Harvard in February and spreads to other colleges
- **Gmail** launches in April with a huge free inbox (invite only at first)
- **Motorola RAZR** becomes the must-have flip phone
- **Nintendo DS** launches
- **Digital cameras** overtake film for most consumers around this period

## 2005

- **YouTube** launches
- **Google Maps** launches with online directions
- **Xbox 360** launches in November
- **Video iPod** plays TV shows and music videos

## 2006

- **Facebook** opens to everyone over 13 in September
- **Twitter** launches
- **Wii and PlayStation 3** launch in November
- **Blu-ray and HD DVD** fight over high-definition discs

## 2007

- **iPhone** launches in June. No app store yet
- **Netflix** begins streaming video alongside DVD by mail
- **Kindle** launches in November
- **Text messaging** becomes the main way young people communicate

## 2008 (courier business era)

- **App Store** opens in July with the iPhone 3G
- **First Android phone** (T-Mobile G1) launches in October
- **Google Chrome** browser launches in September
- **Financial crisis** hits in September
- **Bitcoin** white paper is published in October
- **Courier tools:** BlackBerry or flip phone, printed manifests, GPS units on the dashboard, paper logs, cash and checks for payment. Smartphone apps for dispatch are new and rare
- **This world:** Tony Stark's capture in spring and "I am Iron Man" make advanced tech a public topic

## 2009

- **WhatsApp** launches
- **Bitcoin** network goes live in January
- **Digital TV transition** in the U.S. in June ends analog broadcasts
- **Smartphones** spread fast through iPhone and Android

## 2010

- **iPad** launches in April
- **Instagram** launches in October
- **iPhone 4** adds FaceTime video calls
- **4G LTE** service begins in the U.S. in December
- **Uber** begins service in San Francisco
- **This world:** the Harlem fight between the Hulk and the Abomination is filmed by bystanders on phones

## 2011

- **Siri** launches on the iPhone 4S
- **Snapchat** launches
- **IBM Watson** wins on Jeopardy!

## 2012

- **Tinder** launches
- **Smartphones** are now the default for most adults
- **Ride-share and app-based services** spread through major cities
- **This world:** the Battle of New York in May is filmed from every angle on phones. Chitauri salvage reaches the black market

## 2013

- **PlayStation 4 and Xbox One** launch
- **Google Glass** appears for testers
- **Real world:** Edward Snowden's leaks about government surveillance. In this world the bigger surveillance story is Hydra's Project Insight in 2014

## 2014

- **Apple Pay** launches. Tap-to-pay begins to spread
- **Amazon Echo** launches with Alexa
- **Chip cards** replace stripe-only cards in the U.S. over the next two years
- **This world:** S.H.I.E.L.D. collapses and its files are dumped online

## 2015

- **Apple Watch** launches
- **Windows 10** launches
- **Tesla Autopilot** driver assist arrives

## 2016

- **Pokémon Go** in July
- **Consumer VR** (Oculus Rift, HTC Vive)
- **AirPods**
- **This world:** the Sokovia Accords take effect

## 2017

- **Nintendo Switch**
- **iPhone X** with Face ID
- **TikTok** expands internationally (2017 to 2018)

## 2018

- **Smart speakers and streaming** are in most homes
- **This world:** the Snap. Half of all life vanishes, including half of every workforce. Power, transport and supply chains strain

## 2019 to 2022 (the Blip in this world)

- **Real world:** 5G rollout begins (2019). The COVID-19 pandemic (2020). Video calls everywhere (Zoom). mRNA vaccines (late 2020). ChatGPT (November 2022)
- **This world:** the MCU does not show the pandemic. Consumer tech mostly stalls. Survivors live with empty cities, grief support groups and shortages

## 2023

- **This world:** the vanished return after five years. People come back to phones that no longer work on the network, frozen accounts and homes that were sold
- **Real world:** AI chatbots go mainstream. The story can decide whether these exist

## Quick Reference: How People Reach Each Other

| Years | Main tools |
|---|---|
| 1999 to 2002 | Landlines, pay phones, pagers, email on dial-up, AIM, flip phones for adults |
| 2003 to 2006 | Flip phones for most people, texting, AIM, MySpace, early Facebook at college |
| 2007 to 2010 | Smartphones arrive, texting dominates, Facebook, Twitter |
| 2011 to 2018 | Smartphones for nearly everyone, video calls, Instagram, Snapchat |
| 2018 to 2023 | Blip years. Same tech as 2018 for most survivors |

| Years | Main tools |
|---|---|
| 1999 to 2003 | 35mm film, disposable cameras, early low-resolution digital cameras |
| 2004 to 2008 | Point-and-shoot digital cameras, poor camera phones |
| 2009 onward | Phone cameras replace most point-and-shoots |

| Years | Main tools |
|---|---|
| 1999 to 2013 | Cash, checks, magnetic stripe cards, MetroCards |
| 2014 to 2016 | Chip cards arrive, Apple Pay begins |
| 2017 onward | Tap-to-pay spreads, phone payments common |
