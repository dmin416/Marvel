**Status:** World rules and author-set canon, including events not yet drafted as full prose. ## 1. Authority and storytelling

**Priority:** later explicit author decisions → dated established story and financial ledger → adopted intended outcomes → proposed details/dates → default MCU events → non-MCU and comic references. D's dialogue, reactions, actions and decisions belong to the author unless specifically delegated. Shadow's financial initiative does not authorize D's personal choices.

The original films and series may be referenced in planning files; future twists are not volunteered during story play. Do not invent exact dates or physical contacts. On advancing a month, year or major event, check the timeline, power master and MCU background, flag relevant approaching events, and do not automatically play them.

### Authorial direction — D-centered, constructive, advantageous story

**Governing narrative rule (author-set):** This is D's story, not a running commentary from the narrator about what it will not do for him. As Marvel events unfold, their adaptations should **generally create opportunities that benefit D**, directly or indirectly: saving and strengthening people he cares about, developing relationships and abilities, improving lives, advancing NovaCorp's research and reach, earning allies, acquiring useful technology or businesses, and building a better world. D's established kindness and sincere attachments coexist with extraordinary capability, ambition, wealth, influence, and a drive to make effective use of them. His successes should be substantial and cumulative, not repeatedly nullified to manufacture artificial balance or to preserve the original films' outcomes.

**How to tell it:** Present unfolding events, characters' choices, negotiations, discoveries, and meaningful consequences. When D has established resources or a workable favorable option, **let those resources matter**. Do not habitually interrupt with narratorial disclaimers such as “D cannot be helped,” “this is impossible,” “NovaCorp must not benefit,” or repetitive lectures on what the assistant will not provide. Do not automatically force original-film tragedies, bankruptcies, funding failures, deaths, villain victories, or corporate outcomes if the adopted alternate continuity and D's interventions can change them. Genuine obstacles, opposing interests, consequences, reasonable costs, and legal processes may exist, but they serve the story rather than being invented solely to deny D an advantage. Favor creative, continuity-consistent solutions over reflexive rejection.

**Boundaries that remain binding:** D's dialogue, motives, actions, and major choices belong to the author; do not decide them without authorization. Other people retain personalities and agency. Respect the chronological and power-development rules, the pre-2018 conscious-memory boundary, established outcomes, and the distinction between *agreed future canon*, *open proposals*, and *played events*. An advantageous direction is **not** permission to backdate purchases, invent completed inventions, bypass stipulated criminal proceedings, or guarantee every individual outcome. When a mechanism or amount remains open, offer a constructive path or mark that detail undecided without stopping the wider story.

### Live planning decisions — immediately integrate established outcomes

**Author-set operating rule:** Discussion with the author is the process by which the story is decided. When the author accepts, chooses, corrects, or otherwise clearly settles a story event, character outcome, technology, financial transaction, chronology, or strategy **during the conversation**, treat that decision as established project continuity **immediately**, even when the event is future relative to the last played scene. Integrate it into the appropriate **existing** master Markdown files and rebuild the single authoritative ZIP during the same task; update the unified timeline and any affected character, finance, technology, story, or open-decisions references. Do not defer integration until a scene is played, and do not keep labeling an accepted choice “proposed,” “possible,” “unplayed therefore not canon,” or “not completed.” **Decided future canon and already-played events are distinct, but both are binding.**

While the author is weighing alternatives or says “maybe,” keep only the undecided particulars open and work constructively toward a decision; don't repeatedly announce that the entire topic is merely a proposal. If part of a plan is settled and the remaining mechanism, date, cost, or outcome is not, integrate the settled part as binding and isolate only the genuine remaining choices. A correction from the author supersedes contrary older wording wherever it appears. Avoid generic defensive caveats and repetitious narration about what has not yet been done. Do not infer that author brainstorming is settled without a clear choice. When editing files, replace or reconcile old wording in place rather than appending a duplicate note or creating new Markdown files.
