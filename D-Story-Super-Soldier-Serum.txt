D Story: Captain America and the Super Soldier Serum

Note: This is my own version of the story and its characters. Marvel and other works are inspirations only. References to canon, Raimi or other sources describe the inspiration and are not fixed facts of this story unless a file marks them as approved.

Status: reference and design notes. Nothing here is approved for the story. The only story canon is D-Story-So-Far.txt.

Accuracy note: the one solid canon number is the metabolism line. Every strength figure for the MCU Captain America is a fan estimate, since the films state none. Figures marked "my calculation" are arithmetic from the metabolism line and can be rechecked.

The One Canon Number

* Captain America: The First Avenger, the bar scene after Bucky's fall. Steve says he cannot get drunk and Peggy Carter answers: "Your metabolism burns four times faster than the average person"
* Attribution: IMDb's quotes page and a clip transcript both give the line to Peggy. One fan article reverses it and gives it to Steve. The IMDb version is the more reliable
* This is the only hard physiological figure the films provide, so everything below is built on it

What 4x Metabolism Actually Means

Four Times Faster and Four Times More Efficient

* Approved for this story: the serum makes him four times more efficient as well as four times faster
* Metabolism is the rate at which his body processes fuel. Efficiency is how much useful work it gets out of that fuel. The serum raises both
* A normal human body converts something like 20 to 25 percent of the energy it burns into mechanical work and loses the rest as heat. Four times that efficiency means almost all of it goes into output
* This is what solves the heat problem and the food problem at the same time

Food

* Steve post-serum is about 6 feet 2 inches and roughly 240 pounds, so about 1.88 m and 108 kg
* A normal resting rate for that body is about 2,100 kcal a day and about 3,700 with ordinary activity (my calculation, Mifflin St Jeor)
* Four times the rate at four times the efficiency cancels out. His food need lands close to a normal man of his size, around 3,700 to 5,000 kcal a day, while he gets roughly four times the work out of it
* He still eats a lot because he is a large and very active man. He does not need the 15,000 kcal a day that four times the rate alone would demand
* In sustained combat the figure climbs, though nowhere near as steeply as it would without the efficiency
* Story consequence: he eats like a serious athlete rather than like a furnace. He can live on field rations and does not need a dedicated supply line
* Design note: the alternative version, four times the rate with normal efficiency, needs about 15,000 kcal a day. That is roughly 5 kg of food, more than a Tour de France rider on a hard stage, which makes him dependent on an army to feed him. This story does not use that version

Heat

* Approved for this story: he does not run much hotter than a normal person
* The arithmetic alone would say otherwise. Four times the metabolism would mean roughly 400 watts at rest instead of about 100, which is what moderate exercise produces (my calculation). A body doing that continuously would be visibly hot and in trouble
* The resolution is the efficiency above. Waste heat is what inefficiency produces. A body that wastes almost nothing produces almost no extra heat even while processing fuel four times as fast
* So he is slightly warm to the touch at most. He is comfortable in cold weather, tolerates heat well and does not overheat in armor or in a long fight
* The fuel goes into output and not into radiating warmth, which is the whole point

Alcohol

* Alcohol is cleared by liver enzymes at a fixed rate, roughly one standard drink an hour for an average person
* Efficiency does not help here. Clearing a poison is throughput, not useful work, so the four times rate is what matters
* At four times the rate he clears about four drinks an hour
* So he cannot get drunk by ordinary drinking. He would have to drink faster than four drinks an hour, continuously, which is more than a body can swallow and absorb comfortably
* This is exactly what the film says and it follows from the number

Drugs and anesthesia

* The same clearance applies to everything else: painkillers, sedatives, anesthetics, poisons and sedative darts
* Normal doses wear off about four times faster and normal doses are about four times too weak
* Story consequence: surgery on him needs special dosing. Drugging him needs a dose that would kill a normal person. This is a reliable plot lever and it is rarely used

Healing

* Wound healing is limited by how fast cells divide and lay down collagen. Metabolism drives both
* Roughly four times faster healing: a cut that takes a week heals in about two days, a broken bone in weeks rather than months
* Not regeneration. He does not regrow limbs and he can still be killed
* Story consequence: he recovers between fights in days instead of months, which is why he can fight a war without a long convalescence

Disease and aging

* High metabolic turnover with repair to match means he rarely gets sick and does not accumulate damage the way a normal body does
* Steve looks about 35 after roughly 70 years, though most of that is the ice rather than the serum
* In Endgame's alternate timeline he ages to an old man, so the serum slows aging rather than stopping it

Strength: What the Films Show

No MCU film states a number. These are the feats and the fan estimates around them, all unofficial:

* Civil War: he holds a helicopter from taking off with one hand while gripping a building with the other. The aircraft is an Airbus AS350, roughly 1,100 to 1,250 kg empty and up to about 2,250 kg loaded. Fan estimates of the force needed run to three tonnes equivalent and higher
* Civil War: he holds back Bucky's metal arm with one hand
* Age of Ultron: he catches and redirects a moving motorcycle
* The First Avenger: he throws a motorcycle and flips vehicles
* Endgame: he lifts and wields Mjolnir, which is a worthiness test rather than a strength test
* He survives falls, impacts and blasts that would kill a normal person, which is durability rather than strength

Durability Outruns Strength

* Approved for this story: he is about five strong men in strength and about ten times as robust
* So durability is raised roughly twice as far as strength. Five times on the output side, ten times on the taking-it side
* A normal man's rib breaks under a certain impact. Captain America's takes about ten times that. The same goes for his skull, his joints, his connective tissue and his organs

The Parachute Jump, and Why Ten Times Is the Right Number

* In The Winter Soldier, Steve jumps from a Quinjet with no parachute and goes into the Potomac. He swims out. This is the feat that sets the durability number
* A falling human reaches a terminal velocity of about 55 m/s belly-down. Water at that speed behaves like a solid surface
* An uncontrolled water entry is roughly survivable up to about 20 m/s, which is a fall of about 20 m. Past that it kills people
* Impact energy scales with the square of the speed, so a 55 m/s entry carries about 7.6 times the energy of a 20 m/s one (my calculation)
* Ten times robustness allows about 3.2 times the survivable speed, which is about 63 m/s. That is above terminal velocity
* So ten times is exactly the number that lets him survive a no-parachute fall from any height into water and nothing much more. The feat and the figure agree
* It also explains the shield. Ten times covers falling, impacts and being thrown through things. It does not cover a rifle round, which concentrates its energy into a few square millimetres rather than spreading it over a body
* What he survives: a jump from a Quinjet into the Potomac with no parachute, falls from heights that would break a normal body, being thrown through walls and concrete, car impacts, grenade and energy blasts at close range, Bucky's metal arm at full force and a sustained beating from an armored Iron Man suit
* His bones and connective tissue are the part the serum improved most. That is why he can hit as hard as he can without destroying his own hands and wrists and why he can hold a helicopter without tearing his shoulder out
* Combined with four times healing, this makes him very hard to put down and very fast to get back up. He loses fights and is rarely out of action
* Design note: this is the opposite of D's problem. D's strength outruns his tendons, so his emergency lift can injure him (see D-Story-Peak-Human.txt). The serum raised the frame first, so Captain America has no such ceiling
* Story consequence: beating him is not about out-lifting him. It is about out-ranging him, out-numbering him or hitting him with something that does not care how tough he is

He Can Still Die

* Approved: ten times robust is not invulnerable. A bullet can still kill him. So can a fall from high enough, drowning, fire, a knife in the right place or enough damage at once
* Ten times means a shot that would kill a normal man might not kill him. A graze that would cripple a normal man barely slows him. It does not mean bullets bounce off
* The shield exists because he is not bulletproof. He blocks with it rather than taking hits
* The difference is how the energy arrives. Spread over his whole body he can take an enormous amount, which is why the parachute jump works. Concentrated into a point he cannot, which is why a bullet still matters
* Fate is on his side. Things go his way more often than they should, he survives what should have killed him and he is in the right place at the right moment. That is a property of the story and not of his body
* Design note: this is the useful combination. He is tough enough that ordinary danger does not stop him and fragile enough that danger still means something. A character who cannot be hurt has nothing at stake

Commonly quoted figures, all unofficial:

* About 800 pounds (roughly 360 kg) press, from the comics Official Handbook
* About 1,100 pounds (roughly 500 kg) bench press, from fan articles
* Up to 1,200 pounds as a comics upper limit
* Sources disagree on whether the MCU version is peak human or clearly superhuman. The helicopter scene puts him past peak human

A Working Number for This Story

* Approved: he is roughly five strong men. Not five average men, five strong ones
* Taking a strong trained man at about 180 kg on a floor lift, that puts Captain America around 900 kg controlled
* Range: about 750 to 1,000 kg depending on where a strong man is set
* Short all-out effort with everything behind it: somewhere above that, call it 1,200 to 1,500 kg
* For scale, the real world deadlift record is 505 kg by Hafthor Bjornsson with suit and straps, so 900 kg is about 1.8 times the strongest lift any human has ever made. He can do it repeatedly and one-handed
* The serum raises strength, speed, reflexes, endurance, healing and senses together. Durability is the exception and runs well ahead of the rest (see below)

Where He Sits Against This Story's Cast

Using the figures in D-Story-Monthly-Strength.txt and D-Story-Spider-Abilities.txt:

* A strong trained man: about 180 kg floor lift
* World record deadlift: 505 kg, Hafthor Bjornsson, with suit and straps
* D at 23, trained peak human: base lift 214 kg, max effort 357 kg, emergency 575 kg
* Captain America: about 900 kg controlled, so about 2.5 times D's absolute max effort and well past D's emergency lift, which he can do repeatedly without injury
* Gwen with a single spider dose: about 2,000 kg, so a bit over twice Captain America
* D at 300% spider venom: about 51,800 kg, so roughly sixty times Captain America
* So the serum is far past anything training can reach and still a long way below the spider venom. It sits in its own tier

Why the Serum Matters to the Story

* It is the original of every enhancement program in this world. Oscorp's enhancer, A.I.M.'s Extremis, Hydra's Winter Soldier program and Dr. Noah Burstein's work at Seagate all chase the same thing (see D-Story-World-Economy.txt)
* Norman Osborn's Army contract for a "human performance program" is a super soldier contract in everything but name (Played, September 1999)
* The serum's real lesson is that it worked once, on one man, and nobody since has reproduced it. Everything after it is a worse copy that breaks the subject
* D's symbiote does what the serum does and more, by rebuilding cells directly. That makes it the thing every one of these programs would kill for
