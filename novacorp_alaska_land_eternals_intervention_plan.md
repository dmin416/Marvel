## Additional land acquisition and special-governance reference — Alaska and British Columbia (unplayed planning)

**Scope and authority:** The original Alaska *Eternals* footprint (20,000–75,000 fee-owned acres statewide by about 2024, separate leases/easements, $12B cumulative allocation) remains the active intended plan, not accomplished purchases. The alternatives below broaden possible acquisitions, including British Columbia, but do not silently replace the existing budget, sites, or dates. Florida's historical Reedy Creek arrangement is only an illustration of legislative special districts, not governing D-world precedent. No private land title automatically conveys police, zoning, taxation, election control, or sovereignty.

### How much land one individual or corporation could acquire

| Dimension | Alaska | British Columbia |
|---|---|---|
| Existing transferable private fee title | No general aggregate acreage cap merely because one person/company is the buyer; transaction, seller, ownership and title constraints still apply | No general aggregate acreage cap on accumulating ordinary transferable private parcels; acquisition eligibility, title and restrictions remain relevant |
| Public-land baseline | Most acreage is US federal or State of Alaska; Alaska Native corporation land is a distinct private category | Most acreage is provincial Crown land, not owned by Canada's federal government |
| Public land transfer | No right to nominate and buy arbitrary tracts; state auctions/authorized disposals differ from federal actions; BLM Alaska federal sales are unusually constrained by unresolved land entitlements | Crown grants, leases, licences, and resource tenures require provincial approval; *Land Act* ss.20–21 regulate certain **individual Crown grants or leases greater than 520 hectares** (~1,285 acres), subject to exceptions. This is **not** a maximum aggregate private landholding |
| Scale scenarios | Aggregating 1,000 to 1,000,000 acres of already-transferable land is not categorically banned merely by size; million-acre contiguous availability should not be assumed | Same distinction between theoretical aggregate private ownership and feasible assembly; public-to-private million-acre awards would be extraordinary |
| Nonownership rights | State/federal/private leases, utility corridors, easements and permits give only the rights specified | Crown tenures, leases and rights-of-way likewise do not become fee ownership or governmental powers |

**Alaska negotiations:** Washington can consider only legally authorized federal exchanges, leases, rights-of-way, or transfers of actual federal land. An exceptional transfer may need congressional legislation, but enactment cannot be assumed. Alaska's government separately controls state land dispositions, local-government frameworks and relevant permitting; state land is not interchangeable with BLM land. Respect Alaska Native corporation title, restricted allotments, existing rights and public-access, conservation and subsurface interests. Alaska Constitution Article VIII public resource and public-interest principles remain relevant.

**BC negotiations:** Victoria, rather than Ottawa, is ordinarily the owner and decision-maker for provincial Crown land and the creator of municipal/special governance structures. Ottawa is relevant to actual federal property, federal jurisdiction, funding and approvals. Affected First Nations and Aboriginal/treaty rights under Constitution Act, 1982 s.35 need attention in Crown decisions; a nominally vacant tract does not imply unrestricted disposition. Check federal foreign-investment rules, provincial Crown-grant eligibility, land-owner transparency, environmental reviews and regulated sectors by **story-year**, not a present-day threshold applied retroactively.

### Local government compared to company ownership

- **Alaska:** Possible service area in unorganized borough, incorporated city, and, where criteria and procedures are satisfied, home-rule city; each is a **public body** with residents' legal and electoral rights. A home-rule city generally requires a qualifying permanent population (commonly 400), adequate economy and statutory incorporation steps, not developer fiat.
- **BC:** Improvement districts, mountain resort improvement districts/municipalities, and specially tailored provincial legislative arrangements; examples include Whistler and Sun Peaks. Resort frameworks are purpose-bound, not a general corporate-city exemption. Public governance cannot simply be owned as a subsidiary.
- **Practical assembly:** Map surface/subsurface titles, protected lands, Indigenous rights, access, waterways and easements before selecting sites. Secure a fee-owned development core and separately negotiated utility/water/transport corridors; separately petition for municipal or district powers; fund public services, environmental mitigation, emergency response, school and housing capacity.
- **NovaCorp at D's forecast scale:** Jobs, housing, fusion/energy, hospitals, logistics and ports strengthen a public-benefit negotiation but cannot purchase constitutional immunity. Worker and tenant rights, independent utilities oversight, competition law, taxes, beneficial ownership disclosure, cybersecurity, crisis continuity, public access and cross-border investment screening remain material. Model local direct staff, subsidiary staff and contractors separately. The 2018 Snap and 2023 Blip require distinct continuity plans, not a normal demographic extrapolation.

## 1. What can be bought and what cannot

| Land or right | Mechanism | What NovaCorp controls |
|---|---|---|
| Private acreage, buildings, commercial facilities | Voluntary purchase, title, closing | Fee-simple ownership, subject to taxes, zoning, easements and mineral rights |
| Kenai Peninsula **borough parcels** | Auctions or sales authorized by ordinance | Only parcels conveyed |
| State **over-the-counter** or business parcels | DNR disposal programs | Only the deeded interest |
| State land for a station, dock, pad or right-of-way | **Lease, permit or easement** | Contractual use rights |
| Alaska Native corporation lands | Owner-approved **lease, license, easement or voluntary sale** | Negotiated rights; no compelled sale |
| Existing oilfield services, docks, pads, local firms | Buy a willing business, lease or buy assets | Only purchased assets and contracts |
| **Kenai NWR, Chugach NF, national parks, reserved federal land** | **Not real-estate targets**; only narrow permits or concessions | No title |
| State mineral estate, offshore reserves, severed mineral rights | Separate **regulated lease** | Only the specified rights |

## 7. Conditions not to assume silently

- **Land title:** every acre needs a parcel, seller, terms, closing, access, permitted use, recording and taxes.
- **Minor-age issue:** DNR sets an 18-year minimum for individual purchasers; adult custodians or entities execute contracts until D is of age.
- **Alaska residence:** NovaCorp can operate in Alaska while D lives in New York; changing tax domicile needs a real move.
- **Indigenous communities:** partnership, employment, consultation and enforceable agreements over blanket acquisition.
- **Environmental risk:** contamination, permafrost, erosion, tsunami/earthquake exposure, fish habitat and climate affect value and permits.
- **Permits and workers:** oil, refining, clinical care, air and port operations each need their own authorizations.
- **Detection:** instruments detect physical phenomena, not labeled Celestial signals.
- **New cosmic rules:** Deviant-derived absorption, Pym-scale shrinking, Sersi's permanent transmutation, the trillion-life archive and the Fulcrum partnership are D-world rules.
- **No double counting** in the finance master.
