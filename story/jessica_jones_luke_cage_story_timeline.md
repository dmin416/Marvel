# Jessica Jones, Luke Cage and Kilgrave: D Story Continuity Planning

> **Master file for the Jessica/Luke/Kilgrave arc.** It separates established D-story choices, proposed placements and original comics/television reference. With MCU events on by default, the Netflix-era **Defenders** (Daredevil, Jessica Jones, Luke Cage, Iron Fist) exist in D's New York but the user's D-story choices below override the show's sequence.
>
> **Status:** Jessica's **Midtown attendance and January–February 2000 school interactions are established**. The July 2001 accident is the preferred working plan. **Preferred escape:** Kilgrave compels Jessica to drown Reva Connors, Luke Cage's wife; Jessica believes Reva died but Reva survives unconscious; the shock breaks the control and Kilgrave is hit by a bus as she walks away. **Intended end state:** after a D-assisted legal case, Kilgrave goes to prison and D ensures his horrible death shortly afterward; means and timing open. **Detection rule:** if D physically touches Jessica during or after her Kilgrave ordeal, he **immediately knows something is wrong**.

> **Spoiler rule:** this file may contain original-show details as author notes. The rule is about **conversation**: future story details and show twists are not volunteered to the user while playing or discussing the story. Open questions in Section 7 stay open in the D-world until play resolves them.

## 4. D's contact with Jessica

**Established:** if D brushes past or touches Jessica during or after her mind-control ordeal, **he immediately knows something is wrong with her**, with no conversation or voluntary activation needed.

- **Certain:** D recognizes an abnormal condition on contact.
- **Not defined:** whether he identifies Kilgrave, distinguishes active control from aftermath, or can inspect or undo it.
- **Touch-copying:** available only after D's DNA-manipulation breakthrough (shortly after Gwen's late-2002 bite). By Jessica's adult arc it is available; D copies her biology and abilities on contact **if he chooses** or if he is running his standing "copy everyone I touch" mode. The copy and the recognition are separate effects.
- D may offer comfort, help, an observation or space; recognition does not force intervention. Jessica's response is hers.

**Proposed line, not spoken canon:**

> "I don't know if it's my place to say but I think you're under someone else's control."

### Ways D and Jessica might meet

1. **Unplanned street contact** (preferred): they brush past in New York, perhaps after her escape.
2. **A Midtown acquaintance reconnects** through a classmate or errand.
3. **During captivity:** D brushes past while she still obeys Kilgrave; no rescue is forced.

### How D could reach Kilgrave

1. D touches Jessica and knows something is wrong.
2. He comments or offers support; Jessica answers, refuses or leaves.
3. If she discloses a controlling man, or D finds a lead, Kilgrave becomes a lead.
4. Jessica's investigation, another victim or a witnessed command brings D near Kilgrave.
5. **Only after a played touch of Kilgrave** is a copy of his biology possible. His mechanism, D's susceptibility and use of a copy are unresolved.
6. D chooses whether to avoid, cooperate, comfort, investigate or confront. **Imprisonment and death are intended outcomes.**

Touching Jessica is not touching Kilgrave.

### The Defenders and the wider ensemble

With the Defenders on by default, Daredevil, Luke Cage and Iron Fist may cross paths with D's circle. D, Gwen (Spider-Girl), Peter, Harry, Lorna and Jessica can all pitch in on MCU-era crises when it makes sense, including the slots the MCU gave Spider-Man.

## 5. Luke Cage: open continuity

Luke's birth year, origin, prison experiment, Hero for Hire work, first meeting with Jessica and romantic future are **not fixed**.

**Comics:** Carl Lucas is wrongfully imprisoned, gains strength and toughness through an experiment, becomes Luke Cage, Hero for Hire; he and Jessica eventually have a relationship.

**Television and D-story revision:** Reva Connors is Luke's wife. Kilgrave forces Jessica to drown Reva but **Reva survives unconscious**; Jessica believes she died, which triggers her escape.

### Reva survives: established intended divergence

Jessica drowns Reva under compulsion and believes she has killed her but Reva **does not die**: she loses consciousness and Jessica, who is not a doctor, mistakes it for death.

**Open logistics:** how Reva is found and treated in time; lasting injuries; exact duration and rescuer.

**Consequences:** Reva is alive. When Jessica learns the truth, when Luke learns what happened, whether Luke and Reva stay together and whether Luke and Jessica later become involved are open. The show's widower premise is not imported.

## 8. Possible D-assisted confession: Jessica's informed decision

If Jessica confides in D about Kilgrave, **D can offer to reverse-engineer Kilgrave's mind-control ability and use an adapted version to make Kilgrave confess in his own words**, avoiding the show's convoluted path and its extra deaths.

**Consent is essential.** D asks Jessica whether she wants this, explains it means compelling Kilgrave **against his will** and leaves the choice to her. The comparison is deeply unequal but the tension is real. Her disclosure alone is not consent.

**Mechanism:** after a played touch of Kilgrave (post-unlock), D has a copy of his biology and abilities. Analyzing, reverse-engineering and safely applying it is **not yet worked out**. A forced confession is not automatically admissible.

### Courtroom demonstration: preferred scene concept

1. Kilgrave is brought into court or a formal hearing.
2. Jessica pushes for direct proof, effectively asking him to **show the judge what he can do**.
3. Kilgrave demonstrates shockingly, for example making the judge **take off his pants** and then **commanding him to kill himself**.
4. The judge visibly begins to obey; Jessica cries **"No!"**
5. Kilgrave stops the judge before death (voluntarily, under D's compelled instruction or by another safeguard; undecided).
6. The demonstration supports Jessica's account; it does not by itself prove every charge or guarantee conviction.

**D's role:** he actively helps: securing contact with Kilgrave, setting up public proof, using the adapted power if Jessica agrees and staying close enough to stop a massacre. Jessica stays the central victim and moral decision-maker.

**Procedural gap:** establish containment, why the court allows it, how the judge survives and how the case proceeds. D controlling Kilgrave makes it safer but complicates what it proves.

**Guardrail:** the goal is proof and confession, not reproducing Kilgrave's abuse on bystanders.

### Kilgrave's fate: user-established

After D has acquired Jessica's and Kilgrave's abilities (and those of others he actually touches during these events), **D deliberately ensures Kilgrave dies very shortly after being imprisoned, in a horrible manner**. This replaces the show's ending, where Jessica kills him. Prison, interval, manner and public knowledge are open.

**Sequence:** Jessica's disclosure and decision → D-assisted proof/confession and legal resolution → prison → shortly afterward, Kilgrave's death.

**Agency:** Jessica's consent to the confession is **not** consent to the later death; her knowledge and reaction are not established.

## 9. Continuity audit

**Resolved:** Kilgrave's fate is fixed (prison, then D-caused death). Copying requires a real touch after D's post-bite unlock.

**Outstanding decisions:**

1. **Accident and schooling:** July 2001 and ~January 2002 are working dates; graduating in 2003 needs a recovery and academic plan. With Jessica's whole family dead, any show storyline built around a surviving relative needs adapting.
2. **Comics/TV bridge:** whether the comics' eight months are kept; which other show events (including Hope's story) exist. If the D-assisted case comes before the show's fatal incidents, those tragedies cannot also have happened.
3. **D's recognition:** distinguish active control from aftermath; D cannot know Kilgrave's identity from recognition alone.
4. **Acquiring powers:** separate touch scenes after the unlock.
5. **Kilgrave's mechanics:** how control works here, D's susceptibility, range and limits.
6. **Court and due process:** containment, intervention and admissible evidence.
7. **Consent vs. later action:** kept separate.
8. **Prison timeline:** specified when reached.
9. **Luke's continuity:** origin, discovery of the truth, relationship timing.
