# ORIGINAL MARVEL FILMS: IN-UNIVERSE EVENT DATES REFERENCE

> **How this file applies to D's world:**
> - **Part I (MCU) events happen in D's world by default** on these dates unless D, the author or an established D-world fact changes them. D-world exceptions are listed in the MCU film chronology reference (Spider-Man slots go to the D-world ensemble; Sokovia becomes Latveria; Pietro survives; Snap and Blip happen).
> - **Part II (non-MCU) events are opt-in.** They happen only where the story adopts them (the 2005 Fantastic Four vs. Doom fight and Raimi-inspired Oscorp villains are adopted).
> - **No theatrical release dates.** All dates are the fictional events' own. Non-MCU film worlds are separate source continuities.

**This single master replaces the former standalone MCU timeline and non-MCU selection/list files.** The unified D-world timeline remains the authority for played story events.

**Coverage:** the **22 MCU films through *Endgame***, an **Eternals** supplement and the 31 non-MCU films in the project list. **Marvel Studios' 2023 *Official Timeline* controls MCU dates** over the 2018 *First Ten Years*. Event-level reference, not a scene transcript. **Spoilers throughout.**

## Adopted D-world MCU exceptions

- **Spider-Man:** Peter Parker has no spider powers in D's world. The Spider-Man moments in *Civil War*, *Homecoming*, *Infinity War* and *Endgame* go to whoever makes sense: Gwen (Spider-Girl), Peter, Harry, Jessica, D, Lorna or the Defenders.
- **Sokovia → Latveria:** HYDRA's scepter experiments, Ultron's 2015 city lift, the 2016 **Latverian Accords** and Helmut Zemo are Latverian. The Latverian royal family dies; Doom returns to rule and confront the HYDRA infestation, with D's help after D brokers his understanding with the Fantastic Four.
- **Wanda and Pietro** are latent mutants whose powers are unlocked by the Latverian scepter experiments. **Pietro survives** the 2015 battle.
- **Snap (2018) and Blip (2023):** D and the older child disappear; Lorna and the younger child remain. Both return at the Blip. These are locked author decisions, with the exact farewell mechanics described in `d_world_story_foundation.md`.
- ***The Incredible Hulk*** (spring 2010) is adopted with D's intended gamma/serum sources.
- **Battle of New York (spring 2012)** is the default candidate for Lorna's Aurora display. Lorna has no other superhero alias; **Aurora** is a future nickname only.
- Other D-world changes in the story foundation and world rules also override.

## How to read the dates

| Notation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `1943-06-22` | Specific day from a chronology source; not necessarily on screen. |
| `2016-09` | Month supportable; day not. |
| `spring 2018`, `≈2005` | Season or estimate. |
| `about 16 months after…` | Relative sequence firmer than absolute date. |
| `UNPLACED` | No defensible date. |
| **[A]** | Strong anchor: title card, dialogue, historical event or stated interval. |
| **[B]** | Official or researched chronology; day may be reconstructed. |
| **[C]** | Approximation or fan placement. |
| **[! conflict]** | Genuine unresolved contradiction. |

# Fantastic Four (2005) — Reed Richards' Finances and Doom's Mission Agreement

## Placement in D's Marvel continuity

- **Setting:** Before the Fantastic Four's space mission and cosmic-radiation exposure, provisionally in late 2004–early 2005.
- **Contract timing:** Reed Richards signs Victor von Doom's funding agreement **six weeks before the mission**, as established for this project.
- **Provisional mission date:** January 20, 2005, chosen as a potential real solar-particle-event anchor rather than a confirmed film date.
- **Corresponding provisional contract date:** December 9, 2004. These exact dates are **not final canon**.

## Reed Richards' pre-mission financial difficulties

- **Baxter Building:** Victor mentions that Reed has a **fourth mortgage on the Baxter Building** during their funding discussions.
- **WIRED coverage:** The 2005 film's screenplay features unfavorable coverage of Reed's financial problems, including a WIRED cover referencing bankruptcy and research funding/grant cutbacks.
- **Past venture:** The discussion raises Reed having bankrupted a company or prior venture; **the identity of any company, exact liability and number of failures are not established here**. Do not invent a named company or an amount owed.
- **Funding problem:** Reed has been unable to secure adequate backing for the space mission, including unsuccessful NASA funding, leading him to approach Doom for the financing and station access he needs.

## Victor von Doom's agreement

- **Doom's share:** 75% of the mission's commercial applications and associated patent rights, as established for this project's adaptation.
- **Reed's side:** Retains the remaining 25% economic interest, subject to the contract's intellectual-property provisions.
- **Timing:** Signed six weeks before the mission.
- **Limits:** Ownership of discoveries and patent rights does **not** mean Doom owns Reed, his teammates, or their bodies and biological powers. The precise legal treatment of discoveries produced by their transformations is unresolved.

## Relevance to D and NovaCorp

- D and NovaCorp already exist as major technology and investment actors, but **no approach to Reed, refinancing of his mortgages, purchase of his work, or intervention in the Doom agreement is established**.
- Reed's financing difficulties and Doom's control of commercialization rights could matter to any later NovaCorp involvement; those consequences remain for future story decisions.

## Continuity safeguards

- Preserve the financial distress, fourth mortgage, WIRED coverage, and six-weeks-before-mission agreement.
- Do not invent Reed's debt amount, a bankrupted company's name, new business relationships, or changes to Doom's rights.
- Distinguish the **January 20, 2005 solar event proposal** from the movie's fictional cosmic-radiation cloud; a real flare is not itself proof of the film's date or mechanism.

## Additional pre-mission film details and their implications

### Commercial stakes and Victor's motivations

- Victor believes the mission could greatly increase the value of his company's stock offering; the screenplay specifically has him anticipating approximately **doubling** it if Reed's prediction succeeds.
- The negotiated **75% share of commercial applications and patent rights** is therefore commercially important, not merely an expression of Victor's ego. It gives him an unusually strong claim over research commercialization while Reed's side retains a minority economic interest.
- The movie does **not** put a reliable dollar value on Reed's total debts, the entire mission, or its eventual patents. Do not invent one.

### Existing orbital infrastructure

- Von Doom Industries has access to its own **orbital space station** and launch facilities/spacecraft. The film portrays the venture as having developed sophisticated private aerospace infrastructure before the mission.
- This is a potential comparison point for NovaCorp's aerospace capabilities, but **does not establish that NovaCorp has built a comparable station or entered any deal**.

### The people already working for Victor

- **Susan Storm** is introduced as Von Doom Industries' **Director of Genetic Research**. Her scientific responsibilities are part of her existing employment, separate from her earlier romantic history with Reed.
- **Johnny Storm** is already on Victor's payroll as a pilot. Ben Grimm is Reed's preferred pilot but is not given command of the mission.
- Reed and Victor have a past association at MIT. Reed and Sue were previously romantically involved; Victor is romantically interested in Sue. Preserve these as existing film-character context, not new D-world encounters.

### Scientific purpose and mission hazards

- Reed's aim is to study the cosmic-radiation cloud's possible relationship to the origins and evolution of life, with prospective **genetic and medical applications**, including disease treatment.
- The approaching cosmic event arrives **sooner than Reed predicted**. The catastrophe is a timing/forecasting and shielding problem, not a deliberate plan to give the crew powers.
- The exact fictional radiation mechanism is not explained by the proposed real January 2005 solar flare. Keep that proposed astronomical date separate from the film's internal evidence.

### NASA funding and an unproven allegation

- Reed had already sought backing from NASA without success.
- Ben wonders whether Victor influenced NASA's decision, but this is **Ben's suspicion, not proven sabotage**. Do not state that Victor caused the rejection without a future story decision.

### Financial collapse after the accident

- **Film:** Doom’s investors and financial backers react badly to the failed mission; the screenplay shows collapsing confidence and threats to withdraw support. **D-world addition:** Cecil and seven major shareholders also coordinate deliberate pre-IPO valuation suppression and IPO obstruction to seize Victor’s company at a discount.
